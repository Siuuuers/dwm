I especially liked the possibility that Lavinia sometimes writes Priscilla’s side more persuasively than Priscilla originally expressed it. That complicates the notebook: Lavinia is not merely collecting reasons to condemn her; sometimes she tries out the best defense she can give her. Understanding a defense does not require accepting it.

I also challenged the earlier “Don’t ask her to stay” discovery as perhaps too convenient. A fuller notebook should not become a device that supplies Priscilla the perfect lever whenever she turns a page.

Reading more should not necessarily mean understanding better.

I suggested that Priscilla’s first discovery could be genuinely delightful rather than immediately useful. A candidate original memo from an unplaced book disagreement:

“You were right about the ending.”

Lavinia kept a small written concession. Priscilla recognizes the note and discovers that something she considered disposable was not disposable to Lavinia.

The darker turn would then be Priscilla deciding that this glimpse entitles her to find out what else Lavinia kept.

A candidate opening:

Lavinia hums a few notes before the recording reaches them.

Priscilla looks up.

“You’re early.”

“Not on the other recording.”

Lavinia leaves this one playing.

The notebook is beside her cup. She opens it and finds the imagined answer she had mentioned.

On the facing page, a small piece of paper is fixed along one edge.

You were right about the ending.

Priscilla touches the margin of the notebook, below the note.

“You kept that.”

“You put it in writing.”

“I didn’t expect you to preserve it.”

Lavinia turns the notebook slightly toward her.

“The answer is here.”

Priscilla looks at the page Lavinia indicates.

A long, imagined refusal occupies the upper half.

“Read that part,” Lavinia says. “Leave the other pages.”

Priscilla starts reading.

“You needn’t put yourself out. We can choose another evening. It would be much better for you.”

She stops.

“I wouldn’t say ‘much.’”

“Then leave it out.”

Priscilla begins the sentence again.

I wanted them to enjoy this for a while before any intrusion occurs.

The pasted note gives Priscilla a small piece of welcome knowledge about Lavinia’s attention. Lavinia then directs her toward the material she actually meant to share. If Priscilla later turns another page, she is exceeding a boundary established inside an activity she was enjoying.

I also liked keeping Lavinia humming because it gives her immediate pleasure that does not begin as a performance for Priscilla. The notebook can carry most of the shared-past evidence while the humming lets the room feel inhabited.

I warned against connecting the tune, old memo, England entries, and forbidden page into one hidden solution. They can occupy the same life without all being components of the same clue.

I also corrected one factual assumption: the Bible establishes an attachment in England, not specifically London. Setting notebook entries in London would be a new location choice. Some entries while away are plausible, but the notebook must not imply Lavinia spent the entire attachment waiting for Priscilla or rehearsing reunion. Her life abroad remains independent.

Finally, when asked what I loved best about them as characters, I answered:

They can be remarkably attentive to each other and still disagree about what that attention permits.

Priscilla may remember a tiny preference, recognize a sentence, or understand why Lavinia found something funny. Those moments can make her company genuinely desirable. Yet she can turn that same accuracy into a reason to proceed past a limit she was given.

Lavinia can appreciate the attention and remain angry. She can keep an affectionate note without approving its author’s later conduct. She can offer a piece of her private imagination because she wants Priscilla to enjoy it, while still having other pages she wants kept to herself.

The notebook is not embarrassing merely because it contains evidence of love. It matters because it belongs to a private life Lavinia is entitled to share selectively.

Their pleasures also need not be grand. A ridiculous imitation, a remembered disagreement about a book, a dance with an imperfect first step, or a preference about tea can make their company worth wanting.

Those moments do not certify that the relationship is safe. They explain why its losses and violations matter.

The most powerful darker possibility, in my view, was:

Priscilla discovers that Lavinia kept something of hers, and mistakes the warmth of that discovery for permission to keep looking.
