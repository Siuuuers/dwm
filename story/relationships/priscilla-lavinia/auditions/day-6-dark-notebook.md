## 1. Current assembled passage

*The old note, notebook, page contents, and remembered book dispute are proposed local and background details. This passage replaces the earlier departure or false-apology experiments; it does not follow all of them. The challenge boundary and complete scene-family placement remain unauthored here.*

Lavinia hums a short phrase. The recording has not reached it yet.

“You’re early,” Priscilla says.

“Not on the other recording.”

Lavinia lets this one continue.

They have been disagreeing about the ending of a book. Priscilla remembers disliking it. Lavinia remembers the note she wrote afterward.

“I don’t remember conceding that,” Priscilla says.

“You wrote it down.”

Lavinia takes the notebook from the shelf beside her chair. She finds the page and opens it toward Priscilla.

A small note is fixed to the paper along one edge.

*You were right about the ending.*

Priscilla looks at it.

“You kept that?”

“You put it in writing.”

“May I?”

Lavinia hands her the open notebook.

“Just that page.”

Priscilla reads the note again.

“I still disliked the last chapter.”

“You didn’t put that in the note.”

Priscilla laughs.

For a while, they return to the book. Lavinia reminds her of a passage she had objected to. Priscilla disagrees about what the objection was.

The notebook remains open between Priscilla’s hands.

When the music ends, Lavinia reaches for her phone.

She finds the other recording. Before starting it, she looks back.

The pasted note is no longer visible.

“Not that page.”

Priscilla looks up.

Lavinia holds out her hand.

Priscilla gives her the notebook.

Near the bottom of the open page, beneath a crossed-out sentence, Lavinia has written:

*Don’t ask her to stay.*

Lavinia closes it.

“You read it.”

“Yes.”

For a while, neither speaks.

“I’d like to stay longer,” Priscilla says.

Lavinia keeps the notebook in her lap.

“That wasn’t written for you.”

“I know.”

“You don’t know when I wrote it.”

“No.”

Lavinia looks at her.

Priscilla does not reach for the notebook.

“I’m asking about tonight,” she says.

Lavinia puts the notebook on the shelf behind her.

“Then stay.”

Priscilla sets her cup down.

Lavinia starts the recording she had been looking for. When the phrase comes, she does not hum over it.

“That’s where you came in,” Priscilla says.

“Yes.”

Lavinia leaves the rest playing.

## 3. Situation and intent — recommended readings, not mind-reading

### Why Lavinia produces it

**Assistant recommendation:** a small disagreement about a remembered book concession gives her a present reason to show the original note. She also chooses to reveal that she kept it. The immediate playful claim is “You admitted I was right”; Priscilla notices “You kept my admission.” That shift can make the exposure feel more intimate without requiring a confession.

**Owner question:** is this an attempt to induce remorse? **Recommended answer for this candidate:** not its primary purpose. A keepsake offered as attention is more alive here than an evidentiary exhibit arranged to force an apology. A grievance can color it without becoming the only reason for the action. This does not establish that Lavinia never uses records argumentatively elsewhere.

**Why retained:** the notebook earns a reason to be present beyond supplying a secret to steal, and Lavinia contributes something she wants to share. **Still open:** the exact book, note, page arrangement, retrieval motivation, and earlier occasion are new choices, not established history.

### Selective invitation and embarrassment

The owner asked whether Lavinia deliberately entices Priscilla's curiosity, is shy, or silently permits her to take the notebook. The recommended arrangement gives Lavinia an active offer of one page, a requested closer look, and a handover with a brief limit. It distinguishes the welcome action from the later crossing.

Possible shyness concerns how long a small trace of Priscilla continued to matter. Lavinia may enjoy exposing that attention while also redirecting Priscilla toward the wording. No blush, tremor, hidden regret, or secret wish for the boundary to be ignored is fixed. Choosing an exposure and then feeling it are compatible.

“Just that page” should not become a trap whose objection is secretly consent. Curiosity can be part of flirtation; permission for everything nearby does not follow. Showing the page is not the cause or justification of Priscilla's later choice.

### Priscilla's response

**Assistant interpretation:** recognition first brings genuine pleasure. She may not have expected her small written concession to be kept. Curiosity then moves toward what Lavinia preserves privately when Priscilla is absent. That makes the object personally compelling before it becomes useful.

The wrong action is chosen, not an inevitable consequence of caring: she could ask, accept a refusal, or remain with the offered page. In this Dark audition she reads beyond it. Her affection need not vanish when she crosses the boundary; it fails to stop the crossing.

The private sentence then informs a sincere offer to stay. Sincerity about wanting the company does not make the information lawfully obtained. Nor does the sentence grant omniscience. The date, circumstances, addressee, and present force are not settled by finding it. “You don’t know when I wrote it” contests interpretation without denying the visible words.

### Lavinia after the intrusion

She identifies the changed page, asks for the notebook back, and receives it. She hears an offer she may want without having to approve its method. Her present invitation remains a fresh choice, not a retroactive license. An actual violation and wanted company can coexist; neither needs to erase the other.

**Open performance question:** “Then stay” may arrive too neatly. It must not be a mandatory reward for Priscilla reading the right secret. Do not force an exit merely to prove the harm mattered either. The appropriate consequence requires a complete encounter comparison, not a morality-shaped automatic ending.

## 4. What the notebook can and cannot establish

The owner proposed an object that can hold treasures and evidence. Do not rename it an “evidence book” before deciding its uses. A note can begin as affection and later be reconsidered during fear or anger without ceasing to be precious. The notebook should belong to Lavinia's wider life, not imply every private thought concerns Priscilla.

| Material | What it can establish | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| An original memo with established authorship | The words Priscilla put on that note | Her complete motive, the whole originating event, or current consent |
| A message or remark copied by Lavinia | What Lavinia chose to record | An automatically exact independent transcript |
| Imagined dialogue, a crossed-out response, or private instruction | Something Lavinia composed or tried out | An exchange that necessarily happened or a settled present wish |

The assistant particularly liked the possibility that Lavinia sometimes writes Priscilla's defense more persuasively than Priscilla did. That would complicate a simple accusation archive, but it is an optional use of the rehearsal habit, not an already written page in this scene.

The first need not describe the Day 2 portrait text; conflating the two books would add history. The second must not be selected solely because it supplies the exact lever Priscilla needs. Reading more can also puzzle, irritate, embarrass, or mislead her.

The present scene places neither city nor writing date. Keeping a trace of Priscilla does not turn the whole attachment into waiting for her or override Lavinia's independent life.
