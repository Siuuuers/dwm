Priscilla is reading when Lavinia reaches the table.

She slips a receipt between the pages and closes the book.

“Have I interrupted something?”

“They’re taking out the window.”

“Which window?”

“In the portrait.”

Lavinia sits at the adjoining edge of the table. A small dish of crackers stands between their drinks.

“Start again.”

A woman has commissioned a portrait of her lover. The lover agreed to stay for the sittings. Since then, the chair has been changed, the dress repainted, and the garden enlarged. Now the owner wants the window removed.

“But she wants the garden left in,” Priscilla says.

“Where is the woman sitting?”

“In the room.”

“With the garden behind her.”

“Yes.”

“Put her outside.”

“The owner wants the room.”

“Then keep the window.”

Priscilla opens the book.

“The painter suggests that.”

“Good.”

“She tells him he lacks imagination.”

Lavinia laughs.

“Does he leave?”

“He asks for more money.”

“I like him.”

“He’s already been paid twice.”

“I like him more.”

Priscilla finds the passage and passes her the paperback. Lavinia reads the painter’s objection, then the owner’s reply. The owner has also suggested that the room should look larger without changing the furniture.

“She should have commissioned a house.”

“That would take longer.”

“Perhaps that’s the attraction.”

Lavinia turns the book over.

“You didn’t mention that it was a romance.”

“I said they were lovers.”

“You’ve mostly told me about the furniture.”

“There’s quite a lot of furniture.”

“Does it ever get out of their way?”

“Occasionally.”

Lavinia opens the book again.

“Show me.”

Priscilla finds an earlier page. Lavinia draws her chair nearer to read it.

For a while, neither speaks.

“You could have started here,” Lavinia says.

“You asked about the painter.”

“I’ve changed my mind.”

Priscilla looks at her.

“About him?”

“About what I want to read.”

Priscilla leaves the book with her.

Lavinia reads to the end of the paragraph, then a little further. When she looks up, Priscilla is taking a cracker from the dish.

“Does she ever sit still long enough for him to finish?”

“He did finish.”

“When?”

“Before the alterations.”

Lavinia looks back at the page.

“So what is he doing now?”

“Being paid.”

“And the other woman?”

“She’s still coming for the sittings.”

“Does she know?”

“No.”

Lavinia turns back to the paragraph about the window.

“The owner could ask her to stay.”

“And risk having her leave that afternoon?”

“That explains it.”

Priscilla takes a drink.

Lavinia keeps her place with one finger.

“I thought you’d let me read it first.”

Priscilla puts down her glass.

“You would have wanted it changed back.”

“You could have asked.”

Neither speaks.

Lavinia removes her finger and closes the book.

“Why did you keep finding reasons to come to me?”

Priscilla remains turned toward her.

“I’d rather not answer that.”

“You’ve had plenty to say about her.”

Priscilla looks at the closed book, then back at Lavinia.

“Yes.”

Lavinia waits. Priscilla does not add anything.

“You should have left me alone.”

The paperback stays where Lavinia put it.

She finishes her drink. Priscilla has not returned to hers.

“Will you stay for another half hour?”

“Yes.”

Lavinia tips the snack dish over her palm. A crumb falls out.

“These are less salty.”

“You finished them.”

Lavinia eats the crumb.

“Shall we get some more?”

“Yes,” Priscilla says.

They order another dish and another drink each.

Priscilla puts the paperback beside her bag. Lavinia starts to say something, stops, then asks whether Priscilla has finished reading it.

“No.”

“Don’t tell me the ending.”

“I can’t.”

“Good.”

The next subject takes a little longer to begin.

Priscilla mentions a coat she tried on. It had four pockets, all of them false.

“All four?” Lavinia asks.

“All four.”

“Why?”

“I didn’t ask.”

“Did you buy it?”

“No.”

“You sound disappointed.”

“It fitted very well.”

“Except for your hands.”

Priscilla laughs.

“I kept trying to put them somewhere.”

“Where did they end up?”

“Holding my other coat.”

Lavinia asks her to explain this.

Priscilla had taken off her own coat, tried on the new one, picked the old one up again, and then discovered she could not properly see either sleeve.

“Put it down.”

“I did.”

“Then what was the problem?”

“Every time I went back to the mirror, I tried the pockets again.”

Lavinia laughs before Priscilla finishes.

They spend a little longer discussing what Priscilla actually needed from a coat. Warmth appears surprisingly late in the conversation.

When the new dish arrives, Lavinia takes one cracker and leaves the rest between them.

Priscilla asks her a question. Lavinia answers, then asks one of her own.

The book remains closed.

*The accepted additional interval is fulfilled. Their company continues beyond these represented exchanges; the intervening time contains no hidden confession, reconciliation, or intimate milestone.*

A later subject ends.

“You stayed longer,” Lavinia says.

“You did not ask me to count.”

**The visible passage ends there.** In this proposed occurrence, they eventually leave separately. The cut does not establish an onward date, an overnight visit, or an agreement about the next meeting.
