Tea is on the table. Priscilla’s bag rests beside her chair.

They have been talking over the music. When the track changes, Priscilla looks toward Lavinia’s phone.

“What was the one before this?”

“Do you want it again?”

“Yes. You talked through half of it.”

“You were answering me.”

“I won’t this time.”

Lavinia restarts the track.

They listen. Priscilla takes a drink without looking away from the window. Lavinia leaves the phone beside the teapot.

When the track finishes, Priscilla says, “Again.”

Lavinia returns it to the beginning.

There is little tea left in the pot.

“I had a reply ready in case you said you were busy,” Lavinia says.

“What was it?”

“You have to say you’re busy first.”

“I can’t tonight. I’m sorry.”

“Of course. Don’t worry about it.”

Priscilla lifts her cup.

“Now let me be you,” Lavinia says.

Priscilla puts it down again.

“What do I say?”

“Ask me to come.”

“Would you like to come over tonight?”

Lavinia straightens in her chair.

“You needn’t put yourself out. We can choose another evening. It would be much better for you.”

“I wouldn’t say ‘much.’”

Lavinia repeats the last sentence.

“It would be better for you.”

“Yes.”

“You’d keep the rest?”

“It’s a reasonable suggestion.”

Lavinia laughs.

“Go on, then. You’re me.”

“Of course. Don’t worry about it. I’m sure you’ll have a lovely evening.”

“No. Stop after ‘it.’”

“Too much?”

“Far too much.”

Priscilla laughs.

Lavinia picks up the pot and tilts it over her cup. A little tea comes out.

“You’re making the next one.”

“For what?”

“For adding that last sentence.”

“You asked me to be you.”

“Not to improve me.”

Priscilla sets down her cup.

“All right. Where’s the tea?”

“I’ll show you.”

Lavinia takes the pot. Priscilla picks up the cups and follows her.

At the counter, Lavinia takes the tea from a cupboard and sets it beside the pot.

“The same one?”

“Yes. Like the first pot.”

Priscilla puts in some fresh leaves.

“A little more,” Lavinia says.

Priscilla looks up.

“I tried it your way. I like it stronger now.”

Priscilla adds a little.

“Like that?”

“Yes.”

Priscilla puts the spoon down. Lavinia closes the packet and puts it away.

## Why the two imitations differ

**Assistant interpretation:** Lavinia's Priscilla turns considerate refusal into an arrangement allegedly better for Lavinia. Priscilla removes “much” while leaving the presumption standing. It might be narrow stylistic precision, enjoyment of the caricature, genuine belief in the suggestion, or a mixture. Uncontested wording is not a complete confession of motive.

Priscilla's Lavinia sounds too serenely generous about her absence. Lavinia removes the extra good wish. She may want fidelity to the original script, dislike the false politeness, or want to appear capable of accepting a refusal without appearing indifferent. The dialogue does not prove she hopes Priscilla has a miserable evening.

They recognize something real without fully knowing each other's hearts. The imitation is not an authoritative psychological reading or a reliable prediction of a real rejection. The owner welcomed the imperfect recognition; the assistant cautioned against converting it into omniscience.
