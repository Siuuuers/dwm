The ordinary accepted stay must be inhabited, not represented by a crumb and a closing line alone.

These are local uses of the axes, not their universal definitions. The room, service model, timing, strike transition, fine-motor disturbance, chosen visibility, Group involvement and board access require their existing review. The harm need not be fake because its later visibility is chosen; leaving and refusing contact are not themselves the sinister acts. The interior line about wanting a facial response should be tested by removal, not rescued by making Priscilla cruel enough to make violence seem compulsory.

## Retained pressure and presentation alternatives

The earlier proposal “I was glad you came” makes a different disclosure from “I thought you’d let me read it first.” A partial personal answer would differ from “I’d rather not”: it changes mutual knowledge, not just rhythm. Neither is selected merely by being a smoother transition.

The original novel opening disclosed completion very early; the later portrait discovers it during shared reading. The early question “Would you have told her?” / “Before she agreed?” / “Then she might have gone” was judged a more exposed position-taking turn. Its usefulness and its cost remain available without storing another nearly identical full portrait here.

The hat/haircut version below is different enough to retain as a complete comparison: enthusiasm, deliberate withholding and voluntarily resumed company. It is not a future chapter inside the portrait novel. New scene production still needs the whole family and week, not only this local alternative.

## Earlier complete alternative: the hat, the haircut, and another half-hour

Its independent comic subject and the resumed telling test ordinary company after an explicit refusal. Retain the complete alternate action, not all of its repeated analysis.

Priscilla is explaining the book she is reading.

The man in it has inherited a house. Lavinia has understood that much. He has also bought a hat, although he had apparently gone out to post a letter.

“Has he seen the house?” Lavinia asks.

“Not yet.”

“How far have you read?”

“About a third.”

“And he’s buying hats.”

“One hat.”

Lavinia takes a piece from the snack dish.

“Does the hat matter?”

“I don’t know yet.”

“Does anything happen?”

“He gets his hair cut.”

Priscilla laughs before Lavinia can answer.

“I’m telling it badly.”

“You are.”

“It’s funny when you read it.”

“You’ve made it sound exhausting.”

“It’s also exhausting.”

“Do you like it?”

“Very much.”

Lavinia moves her drink aside.

“Start with the haircut.”

Priscilla has to go back further than that. The man had seen himself in a shop window and decided that the hat did not suit him. Rather than choose another hat, he had gone to the barber.

“With the hat?” Lavinia asks.

“Yes.”

“Did he show it to the barber?”

“He put it on between the cuts.”

Lavinia laughs.

“Did it look better afterward?”

“He thought so.”

“Then I don’t see the problem.”

“He left it there.”

Priscilla reaches for her drink. Lavinia is still considering this.

“I want to hear the barber’s version.”

“So do I.”

They discuss whether the barber should have stopped him. Priscilla thinks he had probably wanted the man out of the chair. Lavinia thinks he could have kept the hat and charged for another haircut when the man returned.

“He might not come back.”

“He’ll need the hat.”

“He could buy another.”

“Then you’ll never finish the book.”

Priscilla puts her glass down.

“That is becoming a concern.”

Lavinia finishes what she has taken from the dish. For a while, neither adds anything.

Then she says, “There’s something I still want to ask you.”

Priscilla looks at her.

“Why did you keep finding reasons to come to me?”

“Your biography was six words over the limit.”

“That was one time.”

“It was true.”

“I did not say it was false.”

Priscilla says nothing.

Lavinia has heard the answer before, or something close enough to it: the occasion presented as the reason. This time she does not begin listing the other occasions.

“I was glad you came.”

Priscilla’s attention remains on her.

“You could have sent a message,” Lavinia says.

“I could.”

“But you came.”

“Yes.”

Lavinia waits.

The conversation at the next table becomes briefly audible. Someone is trying to remember what they ordered.

“You’re not going to tell me why.”

“I’d rather not.”

Lavinia looks down at the dish. There is a broken piece along its edge and crumbs underneath it. She does not take either.

For a while, Priscilla says nothing else.

Lavinia had wanted an answer. She still wants one. Asking the question again would not make it a different question.

Her own glass is empty. Priscilla’s is nearly so.

“Will you stay for another half hour?”

“Yes.”

Lavinia tips the snack dish over her palm. A crumb falls out.

“These are less salty.”

“You finished them.”

Lavinia eats the crumb.

They order another drink each.

For the first few minutes, their conversation is uneven. Lavinia answers one question briefly, then returns to it after Priscilla has let it drop. Priscilla does not bring up the biography again.

Later, it is Priscilla who returns to the book.

“I left out the best part.”

“The house?”

“The hat.”

“Of course.”

“He goes back to the barber for it.”

“And?”

“It’s behind the counter. He thinks it belongs to someone else.”

Lavinia looks at her.

“Does he leave again?”

“He asks where they bought it.”

Priscilla starts laughing before she finishes the account. She has to repeat the barber’s answer.

Lavinia asks whether she is making this part up.

“No.”

“You’ve improved the story since the first version.”

“I told you I was telling it badly.”

“You should have started there.”

“Then you’d have asked why he had a hat.”

“I still don’t know why he has a house.”

“Neither does he.”

Lavinia leans back. Priscilla continues, less concerned now with putting the events in order. Lavinia interrupts when she loses track, and once because she has thought of a worse thing the man could do next.

Priscilla considers it.

“That would be very annoying.”

“You said it was an annoying book.”

“I said it was exhausting.”

Lavinia lets that correction pass.

***

*The accepted interval is fulfilled. Further ordinary company follows. This omission covers time they spend together, not an unseen reconciliation or a skipped change in their relationship.*

A later subject comes to an end.

“You stayed longer,” Lavinia says.

“You did not ask me to count.”

The visible passage ends.

*In fictional continuity, they eventually leave separately. This read-through does not add an onward journey, another appointment, or a private encounter after the bar.*
