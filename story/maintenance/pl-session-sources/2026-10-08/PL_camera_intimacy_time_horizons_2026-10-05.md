## Leading interpretation

Both could initiate flirtation or welcome affection through photography. The strongest current exploration is a shared pretext for desire that both partly recognize, not a camera that automatically authorizes closeness. A practical photographic intention and a personal wish can both be sincere.

Distinguish three cases:

1. **Private cover for one's own desire:** “I want another portrait” can be true while making it easier not to confess wanting another hour together.
2. **Shared flirtation:** both recognize that a pause or a wish to review the images also provides company; neither needs to announce the entire emotional meaning.
3. **A claim against the other person's stated preference:** invoking the picture to demand unwanted contact, more exposure, continued shooting, or a wider interpretation of the relationship. This is a different, consequential act, not the hidden meaning of a playful yes.

A photograph can carry an expression directed at the photographer without proving its complete emotional explanation. Deliberately offered expressions are not automatically less genuine than unposed ones.
