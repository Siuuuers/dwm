## Day 6: The kept page

Lavinia opens the door before Priscilla has put her phone away.

“Come in. The tea’s ready.”

Priscilla puts the phone in her bag and follows her.

Music is playing in the sitting room. Lavinia takes the sofa; Priscilla sits in the chair beside the low table. Her bag rests at her feet.

Lavinia hums a short phrase before the recording reaches it.

“You’re early,” Priscilla says.

“Not on the other recording.”

She leaves this one playing.

Priscilla asks what she has been reading. Lavinia tells her she has started the novel they argued about again.

“You’ve already read it.”

“So have you. It didn’t stop you arguing about the ending.”

“I disliked the last chapter.”

“You agreed with me about the ending.”

“I don’t remember conceding that.”

“You wrote it down.”

Lavinia takes a notebook from the shelf beside her. She finds the page and opens it toward Priscilla.

A small note is fixed to the paper along one edge.

*You were right about the ending.*

Priscilla leans forward.

“May I?”

Lavinia hands her the open notebook.

“Just that page.”

Priscilla reads it. Then she sets her cup down.

“Move over.”

“You can read it from there.”

“I’ve read it.”

Lavinia looks at her, then shifts along the sofa.

Priscilla takes the place beside her with the notebook still open.

“It still says I was right.”

“I didn’t think you’d kept it.”

“It was a useful admission.”

“I’m glad you did.”

Lavinia puts a fingertip beneath the last sentence.

“You could at least read it without arguing.”

“I did read it.”

“Then you argued.”

“I still dislike the chapter.”

Lavinia laughs.

Priscilla begins describing a dinner that occupies much of the last chapter.

“She gives you their names again whenever they come back into the room.”

“You’d forgotten them.”

“Of course I had.”

“Then it’s useful.”

“One of them goes out for a spoon.”

Lavinia laughs.

“And you’d forgotten him?”

“At that point I’d forgotten what they were eating.”

Lavinia tells her. Priscilla disputes that too, until Lavinia reminds her what was brought in afterward.

“Oh. Yes.”

“You can write that down as well.”

Priscilla looks at the old note.

“I think you have enough.”

Lavinia takes her cup. For a while, they continue disagreeing about the dinner. Priscilla remembers a detail Lavinia had forgotten, and Lavinia makes her repeat it.

Later, Priscilla looks back at the note.

“Have you kept anything else?”

“Other admissions?”

“Other things.”

Lavinia leaves a finger against the margin.

“You’re only seeing this one.”

The recording ends.

Lavinia picks up her phone to find the other version. Beside her, a page turns.

When she looks, the pasted note is no longer visible.

“Give it here.”

Priscilla returns the notebook.

Beneath a crossed-out sentence, near the bottom of the open page, Lavinia has written:

*Don’t ask her to stay.*

She closes it.

“Why couldn’t you leave it alone?”

Priscilla looks at her.

As Lavinia moves the notebook to her other side, Priscilla takes her free wrist and brings her palm to the cheek Lavinia struck at the bar.

“Let go.”

Priscilla opens her fingers and lowers her hand.

Lavinia eases her palm away, leaving her fingertips against Priscilla’s cheek.

“Does it still hurt?”

“Not now.”

“You wouldn’t let me before.”

“I want you to now.”

For a moment, Lavinia leaves the contact as it is.

Then she deliberately presses harder.

Priscilla turns her face away and moves back. The contact breaks.

“It does,” Lavinia says.

“Now it does.”

Lavinia lowers her hand.

Priscilla remains seated, a little farther from her than before.

For a while, neither speaks.

“You still haven’t answered,” Lavinia says.

“I wanted to see what else you’d kept.”

“I was showing you.”

Priscilla looks at the closed notebook.

Lavinia puts it on the shelf behind her.

“I don’t want to leave yet,” Priscilla says.

“You were already staying.”

“I still want to.”

Lavinia looks at her. Priscilla does not move closer again.

Then she looks toward the phone.

“I’d like to hear the other recording.”

Lavinia picks it up. She finds the version she meant and starts it.

Priscilla takes her cup from the table.

At the familiar phrase, Lavinia looks toward her.

“This one is slower,” Priscilla says.

“Not everywhere.”

“At the beginning.”

“That’s why I keep coming in early.”

They listen through the rest.

When it finishes, Lavinia gets up with the teapot.

“Would you like some more?”

“Yes.”

She makes another pot. Priscilla remains in the sitting room. When Lavinia returns, she has put the used cups together on the table.

The conversation starts again with the recording. Lavinia explains which part she likes. Priscilla asks about a different passage, and Lavinia has to think before answering.

The notebook stays on the shelf.

They finish the tea. The discussion runs out before the music does, and they listen for a while without replacing it with another subject.

Later, Priscilla gets up. She carries her cup to the counter, then collects her bag.

Lavinia goes with her to the door.

“Good night,” Priscilla says.

“Good night.”

Priscilla leaves.

Back in the sitting room, Lavinia opens the notebook at the pasted note. She reads it once, then puts the book away.
