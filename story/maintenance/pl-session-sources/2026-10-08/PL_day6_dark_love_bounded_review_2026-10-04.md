## Day 6: Sit with me first

Lavinia opens the door with music playing behind her.

Priscilla puts her phone into her bag and comes in.

“Tea?”

“Yes.”

Lavinia takes the sofa. Priscilla sits in the chair beside the low table and leaves her bag at her feet.

A cassette is lying beside the television.

“That one again?” Priscilla asks.

“I was thinking about it.”

“The woman with the banquet.”

“Yes.”

Lavinia pours the tea. For a while, they listen to the music. She hums a phrase before the recording reaches it.

“You’re early.”

“Not on the other recording.”

She lets this one continue.

Priscilla looks toward the cassette again.

“The speech is six minutes long.”

Lavinia looks at her.

“You counted?”

“The second time.”

“And after all that, you agreed with me.”

“About the last shot.”

“No. I remember what you said.”

Lavinia takes the notebook from the shelf beside her. She finds the page and angles it toward Priscilla.

A small note is fixed beside a dated entry.

*You were right about the ending.*

Priscilla leans forward.

“You kept that?”

“You put it in writing.”

Lavinia remembers the first credit appearing while Priscilla was looking at her instead of the screen. The remote had rested on her knee while they kissed. They went back to the scene afterward, still holding hands. When Priscilla finally conceded the point, Lavinia had told her to write it down.

Priscilla sets her cup on the table.

“Move over.”

“You can read it from there.”

“I’ve read it.”

Lavinia looks at her, then shifts along the sofa.

Priscilla takes the place beside her. The open notebook rests across Lavinia’s lap, angled between them.

“It still says I was right.”

“I’m glad you kept it.”

“It was a useful admission.”

“You could have asked me.”

“You’ve just denied it.”

“I qualified it.”

Lavinia laughs.

Priscilla smiles and looks back at the note.

Lavinia begins describing the actress’s final expression. Priscilla remembers a different one, earlier in the same scene. They disagree until Priscilla demonstrates the small movement of her eyebrows.

“No. Before that,” Lavinia says. “When she hears someone coming.”

Priscilla tries again.

Lavinia laughs before she finishes.

“That’s worse.”

“You know which part I mean.”

“I do now.”

While Lavinia is looking at her, Priscilla turns the page.

The note disappears beneath it.

“That isn’t what I was showing you.”

Priscilla’s eyes move down the new page.

There are several lines in Romanian, a reminder about a rehearsal, and a heading in English.

*Another version.*

**PRISCILLA:** I wanted to see you.  
**LAVINIA:** Then why did I have to ask?

Below that:

*Let her say it first this time.*

**PRISCILLA:** I wanted to see you.  
**LAVINIA:**

There is nothing after the second name.

“I never said this,” Priscilla says.

“No.”

Lavinia draws the notebook back onto her lap. Priscilla releases the edge of the page.

“Is that what you wanted me to say?”

“For a while.”

Priscilla turns toward her.

“I did want to see you.”

“Don’t read it back to me.”

“I mean it.”

Lavinia closes the notebook.

“I wanted to hear you say it before you saw it.”

Priscilla opens her mouth, then closes it.

For a while, neither speaks.

“What does the rest say?” Priscilla asks.

“I’m not translating it.”

Lavinia gets up and puts the notebook in a drawer. When she returns, Priscilla is still in the place beside hers.

She sits down, leaving a little space between them.

The recording continues. Neither speaks.

When the track ends, Lavinia puts down her cup and gets up.

“I’m going to bed.”

Priscilla turns toward her.

“Sit with me first.”

“I am tired.”

“We could watch some of the film.”

Priscilla is looking at Lavinia, not at the cassette.

“Not the whole thing.”

“All right.”

Lavinia remains beside the table for a moment. Then she takes the cassette to the player.

The film starts beyond the scene she wants. She searches backward, checks the picture, and goes back a little farther.

Priscilla waits on the sofa.

When Lavinia returns, Priscilla shifts to make room. Lavinia sits close enough for their shoulders to touch.

Priscilla is still looking at her.

“Watch the film.”

Priscilla smiles and turns toward the screen.

The woman in the film is alone in the kitchen. She cuts a piece of the cake she has spent the previous scene refusing to let anyone touch.

A voice calls from another room. The woman looks toward the doorway, annoyed. She puts the plate behind a saucepan, waits a moment, and answers with a smile.

Priscilla laughs.

Lavinia looks up from her cup too late.

“Wait. I missed her face.”

Priscilla begins to explain.

“No, let me see.”

Lavinia holds out the remote. Priscilla takes it and searches back.

When they resume, the woman has not yet cut the cake.

“Leave it,” Lavinia says.

They watch the scene from there.

This time Lavinia laughs at the woman’s first look, before the answer that amused Priscilla.

The film continues.

Lavinia finishes her tea. When she leans forward to put down the cup, Priscilla moves the remote out of the way. Lavinia settles beside her again.

They watch through the next scene. Priscilla makes one observation about the actress, and Lavinia answers without turning away from the screen. Later, Lavinia points out something Priscilla has missed. Neither asks to go back that time.

The notebook remains in the drawer.

After a while, Lavinia reaches for the remote and stops the film.

“That’s enough.”

Priscilla looks at her before getting up.

Lavinia takes the cups to the counter. When she turns back, Priscilla has collected her bag.

They go to the door together.

“Good night,” Priscilla says.

“Good night.”

Priscilla leaves.

Lavinia closes the door. Back in the sitting room, she switches off the television. The cassette is still in the player.
