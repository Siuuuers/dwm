## Day 6: The second version

Lavinia opens the door with music playing behind her.

Priscilla puts her phone into her bag and comes in.

“The tea’s on the table.”

Lavinia takes the sofa. Priscilla sits in the chair beside it, leaving her bag at her feet.

For a while, they listen. Lavinia hums a phrase before the recording reaches it.

“You’re early.”

“Not on the other recording.”

She lets this one continue.

Her laptop is open on the low table. Beside the music window is the page for a film they watched before she went to England.

“Were you going to watch that again?” Priscilla asks.

“I was thinking about it.”

“The woman with the banquet?”

“Yes.”

“She spends the entire film objecting to how everyone eats.”

“That’s not the entire film.”

“It felt like it.”

Lavinia laughs.

“You liked the ending.”

“I said the last shot worked.”

“After I made you watch it again.”

“You talked over it.”

“You weren’t looking.”

Priscilla takes her cup.

Lavinia remembers turning toward her when the first credit appeared. Priscilla had already been watching her. The remote had rested on Lavinia's knee while they kissed. Afterward, they went back to the last scene, still holding hands. Priscilla had conceded that the final shot belonged there.

Lavinia had told her to put that in writing.

“You admitted I was right,” she says.

“About the last shot.”

“No. I remember what you said.”

She takes the notebook from the shelf beside her and finds the page.

A small note is fixed beside a dated entry.

*You were right about the ending.*

Priscilla leans forward. Lavinia angles the page toward her.

“You kept that?”

“You put it in writing.”

Priscilla reads it again. Then she sets down her cup.

“Move over.”

“You can read it from there.”

“I’ve read it.”

Lavinia looks at her, then shifts along the sofa.

Priscilla sits beside her. The notebook rests between them, still open at the note.

“It still says I was right.”

“I didn’t think you’d kept it.”

“It was a useful admission.”

“I’m glad you did.”

Lavinia presses down a loose corner of the note.

“You should make more of them.”

“Written admissions?”

“It would save time.”

“I don’t think it would.”

Lavinia laughs.

They return to the film. Priscilla still dislikes the woman's speech at the banquet.

“She keeps saying she won't take up their time.”

“She doesn't.”

“She talks for six minutes.”

Lavinia turns toward her.

“You counted?”

“The second time.”

“You could have said you didn't want to watch it again.”

“I did want to watch it again.”

For a moment, Lavinia looks at her rather than the notebook.

“You were watching that time.”

“Yes.”

The music changes.

Lavinia reaches for the laptop to lower the volume. Beside her, paper moves.

Priscilla has turned the page.

There is a rehearsal reminder in the margin and several lines in Romanian. Beneath them, Lavinia has written a heading in English.

*Another version.*

**PRISCILLA:** I wanted to see you.  
**LAVINIA:** Then why did I have to ask?

Lower down:

*Let her say it first this time.*

**PRISCILLA:** I wanted to see you.  
**LAVINIA:**

The space after her name is empty.

“That isn't what I was showing you.”

Priscilla's eyes move down to the second exchange.

“I never said this.”

“No.”

Lavinia draws the notebook back onto her lap. Priscilla does not resist.

“Is that what you wanted me to say?”

“For a while.”

Priscilla looks at her.

“I did want to see you.”

“Don't read it back to me.”

“I mean it.”

Lavinia closes the notebook.

“I wanted to hear you say it before you saw it.”

Priscilla begins to speak, then stops.

The music is still playing. Lavinia can hear the words, but she has lost where they are in the song.

Priscilla looks toward the notebook.

“And underneath?”

“What?”

“The part I couldn't read.”

“I'm not translating it.”

Priscilla turns back toward her.

Lavinia gets up with the notebook. She puts it in a drawer and closes it.

When she returns, Priscilla has moved her own cup away from the edge of the table. She is still in the place beside Lavinia's.

Lavinia sits.

For a while, neither speaks.

“Could we watch some of it?” Priscilla asks.

Lavinia looks at the laptop.

“The film?”

“Yes.”

“Not all of it.”

“All right.”

Lavinia stops the music. She starts the film near its opening and moves the laptop so they can both see.

The woman from the banquet is alone in a kitchen. She straightens a row of plates, opens a cupboard, and finds the cake she will later forbid everyone to touch.

Priscilla watches.

“There,” Lavinia says.

“I see it.”

“That's what I was trying to show you.”

The woman cuts a slice and eats it standing up. When someone calls from another room, she puts the plate behind a saucepan before answering.

Priscilla laughs.

Lavinia does too, a moment later.

They watch through the next scene.

“I'd forgotten how quickly she answers,” Priscilla says.

“She doesn't want them coming in.”

“I know. I meant the actress.”

Lavinia looks back at the screen. She lets the scene continue rather than replaying it.

After a while, she pauses it and reaches for her cup.

Priscilla remains looking at the stopped image.

“What?”

“The woman at the back. I hadn't noticed her.”

Lavinia moves the film back a short way.

The woman is almost out of the frame when they start watching again. Both turn toward the screen.

Later, Lavinia stops the film herself.

“That's enough.”

Priscilla leans back.

“More tea?” Lavinia asks.

“Yes.”

Lavinia takes the pot to the counter. Priscilla brings the cups.

They make another pot. Lavinia chooses a different recording while it stands, and Priscilla asks her to leave it at the beginning.

They return to the sofa with their cups. The drawer stays closed.

Priscilla asks where Lavinia found this recording. Lavinia tells her about the version she heard first, then changes her mind about which one that was. She names another, describes a voice Priscilla does not recognize, and gives up trying to place the order.

“I can find it another time.”

“You don't have to find it now.”

“I wasn't going to.”

Priscilla smiles.

The conversation continues in short stretches, with music between them. Priscilla does not ask about the diary again.

Eventually, Lavinia puts down her empty cup.

“I'm going to bed.”

Priscilla sets her cup beside it and gets up. She takes her bag from the chair.

Lavinia walks with her to the door.

“Good night.”

“Good night.”

Priscilla leaves.

Back in the sitting room, Lavinia closes the laptop. She opens the drawer and takes out the notebook.

She finds the pasted note. For a moment, her finger rests beneath the sentence.

She closes the notebook without turning to the imagined conversation and puts it away.
