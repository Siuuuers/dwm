## 1. The empty display

Sylvia straightens the card beside the empty plinth.

“There.”

A cream rectangle. Two black lines. Above it, the white surface is clean except for a grey crescent near the back.

“It was straight before.”

“It wasn't convincing.”

Beside the door, a woman presses a label flat against the wall. One corner lifts when she takes her thumb away. She presses it again.

“What have you written?”

Sylvia holds the pen behind her back.

“Go and read it.”

*An Improvement.*

Underneath: *Please do not touch.*

“There's nothing to touch.”

“Then they should find it easy.”

The laugh comes out too loudly. Sylvia's mouth opens; she catches the sound against the back of her hand.

“It was empty.”

“I wanted to see what you'd do.”

She draws the pen cap between her fingers. It clicks against a nail.

“You could have said that.”

“You might have prepared.”

“Stand here.”

Her hand closes around the sleeve and draws me half a step to the left. From here, the light lies across the whole top of the plinth.

“Better?”

“There's more of it.”

She lets go of the sleeve. The side of her hand stays against it.

Across the room, a man steps back from a painting. His heel reaches the edge of the carpet before he stops.

“Is this part of it?”

“She helped.”

“I'm sorry. The sculpture for that stand hasn't arrived.”

## 3A. Default: the walk with Lavinia

“Another evening?”

“Perhaps.”

Sylvia adjusts the bag on her shoulder. At the corner she turns towards the cinema; the library is the other way.

At six, the paper around the flowers is damp beneath my thumb.

Lavinia eases one petal out of a fold.

“You're crushing them.”

“I'm trying to give them to you.”

“Then let go.”

The paper slides through the fingers. A small white flower stays caught against a nail. She takes that too and pushes the stem back into the bunch.

“There.”

“You're welcome.”

She lifts the flowers to her face. Above them, the eyes have almost disappeared.

“Where are we going?”

“Left.”

We pass the end of the library. Lavinia keeps the flowers under her nose while I point out the path between the buildings.

“Which way?”

I point again.

“Say it. I can't see through these.”

“Right.”

She lowers them, laughing.

The library comes back into view from the other side.

“That was quick.”

“I know a longer way.”

“I'm sure you do.”

She puts the flowers in the crook of her arm and takes my hand. This time we pass the library steps.

Beyond the side door, the paving narrows. A bicycle leans across half of it. Lavinia turns sideways; the flowers brush the bell, which gives a small, unfinished ring.

“Excuse us,” she tells it.

“You could have gone round.”

“It could have parked somewhere else.”

She keeps hold of the hand behind her until the path widens.

“Priscilla!”

Two people by the gate. One lifts an arm: the woman from the seminar, a cardigan hanging from her other hand.

“We're going for coffee. Are you two coming?”

“Shall we?” I ask.

Lavinia looks towards them.

“If you like.”

“We could come for a little—”

The grip tightens until the knuckles ache. The flowers tilt across the joined hands; one white head trembles against the paper.

The woman waits, smiling. Her cardigan has slipped until a sleeve touches the paving.

“Another evening,” I say. “I'd like that.”

“Send me a message.”

“I will.”

They turn towards the café. The sleeve swings clear of the ground.

“Let go.”

Lavinia's eyes return to my face. The hand opens.

For a moment, the fingers stay together. Separating them brings the ache back.

“That hurt.”

“You were going to take me with you.”

“I thought you might enjoy it.”

“Watching you enjoy yourself?”

“You were doing that a minute ago.”

She shifts the flowers higher against her chest.

“I wanted an evening with you.”

“So did I.”

“Then why do you keep looking after her?”

The gate has swung back into place. Through the bars, the café sign is still legible.

“I was talking to you.”

“You're very good at doing both.”

“Would you prefer me to be unpleasant?”

“For once.”

“All right. That was nasty.”

Her mouth tightens. Then she laughs, too loudly for the small space between us.

“There you are.”

“I'm not laughing.”

The laugh stops. A loose edge of paper moves against her coat.

“What do you want to do?” she asks.

“Keep walking.”

“With me?”

“Yes. And I'm still going to have coffee with her another evening.”

“You haven't even arranged it.”

“You heard me say I would.”

“Then arrange it.”

“Later.”

I start along the path. Lavinia falls into step beside me, the paper crackling at every second stride.

“They're getting in the way,” she says.

“Give them back.”

She moves them to her other arm.

“No.”

The next stretch of path is wide enough for both of us. Her shoulder brushes mine; then again, without the path becoming any narrower.

**End this observation here.** They continue the wanted walk. Priscilla declined this evening's coffee and said she would send a message about another evening; no such message or later appointment is yet shown. No departure time or next-day plan is supplied.
