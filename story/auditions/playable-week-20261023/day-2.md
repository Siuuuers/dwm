## 1. A Longer Evening

Lavinia closes the door on the music from the bar. Her coat goes over the back of the chair.

She sits at the corner of the table, close enough that one menu will do.

Her fingers brush the cuff of my right sleeve before she opens the card.

“Still damp.”

“A little.”

“You should have taken your coat off.”

“I'm fine.”

She looks down at the menu.

“I thought you were only staying for a drink.”

“Have you eaten?”

“No.”

“Then choose something.”

“Do you want dinner?”

“I was hoping to.”

“The mushroom tart.”

Lavinia turns the page.

“Or the chicken.”

The page comes back. Her finger finds the tart again.

“I'll have the chicken.”

“You don't have to.”

“I want to try it.”

“So do I.”

“I noticed.”

After we order, she rests her elbows on the table.

“You can relax.”

The shoulders settle against the chair. A little farther down; the edge of the table rises toward the chin.

“Not that much.”

“You're difficult to please.”

“You haven't tried very hard.”

Another inch. The chair creaks.

Lavinia laughs and catches my wrist.

“Come back.”

I sit up. Her fingers remain around the wrist until the food arrives.

For a while, we eat. She pushes her plate toward me.

“Try that.”

### Food Option: One `Yes` or the Default

**Optional thought:** I could ask her to feed me.

#### If `Yes` is taken

“Give me a bite.”

“You have a fork.”

“I know.”

Lavinia cuts a smaller piece from the tart.

“Come here, then.”

I lean forward. She holds the fork still.

“Careful. It's hot.”

When I sit back, she pushes the plate into the space between us.

“Now eat your own.”

#### If the opportunity passes (Default)

“I should have ordered yours.”

“You're eating it.”

I take another piece before she draws the plate back.

“Some of it.”

She leaves it between us.

### The Dinner Rejoins

Her elbow stays on the corner of the wood.

“What?” I ask.

“I told you yesterday not to look too pleased.”

“This is my ordinary face.”

“It isn't.”

Her thumb touches the corner of my mouth, takes a flake of pastry from the skin, and returns to her glass.

“I could take you out tomorrow.”

“You could let me finish this.”

“The day after, then.”

Lavinia puts her fork down.

“Who had all this while I was away?”

“You aren't away now.”

Her hand opens between the plates. I put mine into it.

The thumb passes over one knuckle, then rests.

“Did you want me to stay?”

“You are staying.”

“Before I went.”

## 3. Alternative Continuations (`A–E`)

### 3A — Never started: an admission kept small

“Of course I did.”

“Then why do I have to ask?”

“I wanted you here, Lavinia.”

Her thumb passes over the knuckle once more.

“I asked you to check the English.”

“You'd left a line out.”

The hand slips out from under mine.

“I'm not giving you anything else to send.”

“I could still look at it.”

“No.”

“The wording wasn't—”

“No.”

I pick up my glass. She waits until I put it down.

“All right.”

Lavinia returns to her food. For a while there is only the cutlery and the music through the door.

I push my plate toward her.

“Was that the last piece?”

“No.”

She takes it.

When she has finished, she puts her fork down beside the knife.

“I should go.”

“I could walk with you.”

“I want to go on my own.”

I draw my chair in so she can get past.

At the door she stops to put on her coat.

“Tell me when you get home.”

“No. You'll ask whether I'm going to bed.”

“Will you be?”

“There.”

She opens the door before I can answer.

### 3B — Started, unfinished: the answer remains unsaid

“I don't want to start that tonight.”

“I'm asking about you.”

“I know.”

Her hand stays open beneath mine.

“Then?”

I turn my hand over and set it beside the plate.

Lavinia folds her fingers into her palm.

The edge of the menu has gone soft where it touches the wet glass.

“Stay until we've finished eating.”

Lavinia draws her plate toward her.

She eats the last piece without offering it. I take a drink.

When I put the glass down, she has her coat over her arm.

“Lavinia.”

She waits.

The folded menu is still between the glasses.

“Nothing.”

She leaves.

### 3C — Won, not Perfect: more dinner

“Yes. I wanted you here.”

“And you decided I should be.”

“I wanted it to be possible.”

“You knew what I wanted.”

“Yes.”

Lavinia begins to draw her hand away. I open mine.

She rests her hand beside the plate.

“I'm not giving you anything else to send.”

“All right.”

“You'd still want to change it.”

“Probably.”

“Priscilla.”

“I'm not asking for it.”

She turns her glass. I wait until she has taken a drink.

Lavinia reaches behind her for the coat.

“Stay a little longer.”

“Doing what?”

“Having dinner with me.”

“We've been doing that.”

“I like it.”

For a moment she holds the glass against her lower lip.

Then she puts it down.

“Until we finish.”

Her hand leaves the coat.

I cut another piece of chicken.

Lavinia watches the knife come down again.

“That's small enough.”

“Is it?”

“Eat it.”

I do. She moves her chair closer to reach my plate and takes the other piece.

For a while, neither of us says anything.

When the plates are empty, she reaches for her coat. I stay in my chair.

“Tell me when you get home.”

“I will.”

She rests her hand on my shoulder while she puts her coat on. I turn toward it.

The pressure lifts.

“Good night, Priscilla.”

“Good night.”

### 3D — Lost: the strike and the threshold self-talk

“Did you want me to stay?”

“Yes.”

Her thumb does not move.

“I wanted you here.”

“I asked you to check the English.”

“You'd left a line out.”

“On purpose.”

“I know.”

Lavinia takes her hand away.

“Would you do it again?”

“I'd show you first.”

“And if I told you not to send it?”

“Then I wouldn't send it.”

“You mean that?”

“If you don't want me to.”

She turns the glass by its stem. A wet crescent remains on the table.

“You'd find something else.”

“You wouldn't have stopped on your own.”

“To keep me here.”

“That too.”

The glass stops.

“Is that supposed to make me feel better?”

“You asked me.”

Lavinia gets up. She takes the coat from her chair and folds it over her arm.

“I'll do it differently.”

“You're still talking about doing it.”

“I'd rather not have to.”

Her palm strikes my cheek.

The hand is down again, open beside the coat.

The cheek burns beneath my palm. Lavinia's eyes stay on my mouth.

“Why are you smiling?”

**Self-talk:** Not indifferent, then.

“I didn't know you were so angry.”

“Well, now you do.”

She turns toward the door.

“Sit down.”

“No.”

“We can stop arguing.”

“I'm going.”

I stand in the gap between the table and the door.

“Move.”

“Just give me a minute.”

“I have.”

The handle is behind my wrist.

“I'm not angry with you.”

“That isn't what I asked.”

I step away.
