## 1. Beat 0 — The Gold Clasp & The Racing Pulse Under a `"Cold"` Wrist

Outside the studio, visitors crowd the corridor with damp programmes.

Inside the narrow warm-up room behind the stage, the crowd noise drops to a muffled hum through the double acoustic doors.

Beside the dressing counter, Lavinia stands with her weight settled into her left hip. Her right heel stays half an inch clear of the floorboards—the exact guarded transfer from before she went away, when she insisted to the department that nothing was wrong.

She holds her left wrist out between us. A thin gold chain hangs open across the bone, the tiny spring ring slipping from her thumb.

“My hands are cold,” she says without looking up. “Do the clasp.”

Two fingers take the gold link. Beneath my thumb, the skin on the inside of her wrist is warm, not cold at all; the pulse beats fast and shallow just beneath the tendon before she has even stepped back onto the floor.

The spring ring clicks shut.

Instead of letting go, my thumb passes once over the closed gold clasp and rests in the hollow of her wrist.

Lavinia does not draw her arm back.

“You're taking a long time over one catch,” she says softly.

“It's small.”

“Your hands aren't shaking.”

“Yours aren't cold.”

Her fingers curl once around the back of my wrist—holding my thumb against her pulse for the length of a breath—before she lets her arm fall to her side.

## 2. Beat 1 — Sylvia's Lower-Gate Card & The Second Piece (Optional Thought: Single `Yes` vs. Default)

On the dressing counter beside the rosin box lies a folded white card in Sylvia's neat, left-handed diagonal crease:

*Quiet rest room reserved downstairs. Key at the desk if you want to leave by the lower gate.*

Lavinia sees where my gaze has stopped.

“She left that before the first piece,” Lavinia says. “And the visitors in the front row asked for the second variation.”

“The programme only promised one.”

“I told the rehearsal director I'd do the second.”

“With your right heel off the floor?”

“It won't take long.”

She bends toward the rosin box. Before her fingers reach the wood, her right knee gives a small, silent tremor under the skirt; her palm catches the edge of the counter and stays there a beat longer than the reach requires.

“Don't say a word,” she says.

“Nothing has been said.”

She straightens and looks toward the rear exit glass, where rain is streaking the courtyard light.

“After the second piece,” she says, her English flat and clear, “I'm taking the lower gate by myself. Not the bar. Not the flat. I want one evening where nobody is writing my schedule.”

**Optional thought:** Cross the second piece off the stage-door board while her back is turned.

*Control: one **Yes** button. Ignoring it lets the opportunity expire into the default below.*

### If `Yes` is taken

While Lavinia adjusts the cardigan over her shoulders, two silent steps reach the clipboard hanging beside the inner studio door.

In the *Second Variation / Floor Q&A* row, one clean stroke of the pen crosses out *Second Variation* and writes beside it:

*Session concluded. Clear hall.*

Behind her back, the pen cap clicks shut.

“What did you just write?” Lavinia asks, turning around.

“The visitor count exceeded the room limit. They have to clear the studio after the interlude.”

“There are twenty people out there.”

“Twenty-four. The fire notice says twenty.”

### If the opportunity passes (Default)

The clipboard stays untouched on the hook; two steps place my shoulder directly between Lavinia and the inner studio door.

“The stage manager already moved the next slot to the main hall,” I say.

“He didn't tell me.”

“He asked me to tell you.”

Lavinia looks down at my empty hands, then up at my mouth.

“You're lying.”

“Go out and ask him. You'll have to walk across the floor on that right leg in front of twenty-four people to do it.”

## 3. Beat 2 — The Breaking Point: Costly Admission, Extra Disclosure & Corrective Tenderness (`7A–7E`)

Through the double acoustic panel, applause rises and thins as the pianist finishes the interlude. In a moment the stage manager will check the door.

Lavinia takes one step toward the brass push-bar. Her right heel touches the floorboards, hesitates for a fraction of a second, and takes her weight anyway.

“Move, Priscilla,” she says. “I'm doing the second piece, and then I'm going home alone.”

**Self-talk — Challenge impulse:** Hold the door shut and give her the truth she's been trying to tear out of me all week.

“You really can't leave a single thing alone, can you?”

“Not when it's you.”

### 7B — Started, unfinished: the weight on the right foot

My hand lifts toward her sleeve, then stops in the six inches of air between us without touching the wool.

“Go on, then. Push the door open.”

Lavinia looks at my stopped hand, then at the heavy acoustic door.

To depress the bar and swing the soundproof panel outward, she has to plant her right foot flat and drive her weight through the ankle. She shifts her stance once—and stops.

In the narrow wire-glass strip of the door, her reflection watches mine.

Through the wall, the stage manager thanks the visitors for attending and directs them toward the foyer. The window for the second piece closes while neither of us moves.

Lavinia lets out a short, quiet breath that sounds almost like a laugh.

“You knew I'd wait a second too long,” she says, and reaches for her coat.

### 7C — Won, not Perfect: the costly admission on the bench

“Don't open it,” I say quietly. “It wasn't only because of your leg.”

Lavinia's fingers stop on the brass push-bar. She does not turn around right away.

“What did you say?”

“Before you went away. I told myself it was for your leg, and I told the department it was for your leg. But I sent the amendment without showing you because I knew that if you read it first, you would still get on the plane and leave me behind.”

Slowly, Lavinia turns her back to the acoustic door. Through the thick wood behind her shoulder, the stage manager announces that the session has ended. Neither of us looks toward the hall.

“You made me wait four months to hear you say that without an excuse,” she whispers.

“I'm saying it now. Sit down and let me take your ribbons off.”

She sinks onto the wooden bench and reaches down with unsteady fingers; I kneel on the floorboards in front of her, untie the pale satin from her right ankle, and slip her street shoe onto her foot before we walk out to the rear door.

*(Proceed directly to **Section 4**.)*

### 7D — Lost: the costly admission, the extra disclosure & corrective tenderness at the door (With Self-Talk Selector & Default)

Before her fingertips touch the brass push-bar, my right hand closes around her left wrist—directly over the thin gold bracelet clasp—and draws her arm down from the door. My left palm plants flat against the heavy acoustic panel beside her cheek, holding the door shut with my weight.

Lavinia's breath catches hard.

“Let go.”

“No.”

“Tell me right now that you sent that form for my leg,” she whispers fiercely against my mouth, her free hand twisting into the wool at my lapel. “Go on. Use your committee voice one more time.”

“It wasn't for your leg,” I whisper against her lips, every trace of polish gone, my voice softer than in the library and completely still. “You could have danced on it for another month. I altered the form because I couldn't bear the thought of someone else watching you in another city while I stayed here.”

Against the heavy wood, Lavinia's shoulders go rigid; her eyes widen an inch from mine.

“That's all?” she breathes.

“No,” I whisper, sliding my right hand from her gold bracelet to lace tightly between her fingers and pressing our joined hands flat against the door panel beside her hip. “When you asked for an answer on Monday, I recorded Sylvia reading a line out of her novel and sent it to your phone so you wouldn't hear how badly I wanted you back. And two minutes ago I crossed your second piece off the stage manager's board behind your back.”

Through the thick soundproof panel two inches behind her head, the stage manager's voice announces to the crowd that the performance has concluded. Chairs scrape back; twenty-four pairs of footsteps file out into the corridor and fade away.

The second piece is gone.

Lavinia does not call out through the door, and she does not let go of my lapel. Instead, her free hand slides up from my collar into the hair at the back of my neck, her fingers locking hard at my nape as she pulls my mouth down to hers—her breath hot, trembling, and electric against my lips.

“*Ești un monstru,*” she breathes against my mouth—and then in English, her forehead dropping hard against my collarbone: “You're completely monstrous.”

## 4. Beat 3 — The Single Identically Presented Dark Ending (Common to All P–L Runs)

*(Presented identically across every Priscilla–Lavinia traversal. After softer local choices (`7A/7B/7C/7E`), its calm domesticity reads like an eerie, unspoken surrender; after `7D`, every gesture carries the chilling weight of the confession and containment at the door two minutes earlier.)*

At the rear doors of the building, the evening rain falls straight down through the courtyard lamp light.

The long black umbrella stands in the rack beside the threshold.

My right hand takes the curved wooden handle; my shoulder pushes the heavy door outward, and my thumb presses the catch.

The black canopy opens with a soft *thrum* against the wet air.

On the stone threshold, Lavinia buttons her coat to the chin. The heavy canvas bag hangs from her right shoulder.

Without asking, my left hand slips the canvas strap off her shoulder and settles it over my own arm.

Then I extend the umbrella over the top step—the wooden handle held in my right hand, tilting the canopy three inches toward my own side.

On the wet stone, the dry circle is barely wide enough for two pairs of shoes.

Lavinia looks at the rain falling past the black rim, then at the narrow dry space flush against my dry coat.

“That isn't very wide,” she says.

“It's the same umbrella as Tuesday.”

“You're holding it differently.”

“Come here.”

For a second she stands under the stone lintel.

Then she steps out under the canopy—close enough that her left arm presses the full length of my coat from shoulder to hip, her right foot taking the first step down with no weight on the turn.

At the bottom of the walk, where the hillside path divides toward the station and the flats, she does not look toward the station.

Her left hand slips inside the pocket of my coat, where five fingers are already waiting to close around hers.

“What time are you coming tomorrow?” she asks.

“Whenever you wake up.”

“That isn't a time.”

“Before then.”

Lavinia laughs once under her breath, rests her temple against my shoulder beneath the black canopy, and lets the turn take us up the hill.

**End of the Priscilla–Lavinia chapter.**
