## 1. A photograph

Sylvia holds her phone out. I move my chair until both our faces fit in the screen.

“Not that expression,” she says.

“It's my expression.”

“It's the one you use when the university might publish it.”

I smile more broadly.

“Worse.”

My own phone sounds on the table.

**Lavinia, message:** Try not to look too pleased when you see me tomorrow.

I read it again.

Sylvia takes the photograph.

“There.”

She brings her phone between us. In the picture, I am looking slightly below the lens. Sylvia has caught herself watching me.

“Who was it?”

“Lavinia.”

“I should have asked her to help earlier.”

I enlarge the picture with two fingers. Sylvia leans against my arm to see what I am doing.

“Don't inspect it that closely.”

“I like it.”

“That isn't usually why you zoom in.”

**Self-talk:** I like her face in this one, too.

### Photo option: one Yes or the default

**Optional thought:** Ask for another photograph.

#### If Yes is taken

“Wait. One more.”

“We have a good one.”

“You aren't looking at the camera.”

“I was supervising.”

I pull my chair closer. This time I watch the screen instead of my phone.

Sylvia tries to arrange her mouth, looks across the screen, and laughs before she takes the picture.

“Now you aren't looking at the camera either,” she says.

“Send me both.”

She does.

#### If the opportunity passes

“Send it to me.”

“Without making me take six more?”

“While you still like me.”

Sylvia sends the photograph, then checks it once herself.

“You can have one more later. If necessary.”

### The photograph passage rejoins

Sylvia puts her phone away and moves her chair to the other side of the table.

The paperback beneath her bag has a bent cover. She smooths it with her palm.

“Bought it on the way here,” she says. “It looked awful.”

“That's a recommendation?”

“For this, yes.”

She opens it at a folded page.

## 2. A reading

Sylvia lowers her voice.

“‘If you leave this house,’ she said, ‘I shall never look at you again.’”

“Is he at the door?”

“Foot of the stairs.”

“Then she's early.”

“Would you like to do her?”

“No. Go on.”

Sylvia puts one hand against her chest.

“‘My cousin required the carriage.’”

I laugh before she has finished the last word.

“What?”

“The voice. Is he ill?”

“He's in love.”

“Give me his part.”

She turns the book toward me, keeping a finger beneath the line. I read his next speech in the same appalling voice.

Sylvia bends over the table. I have to wait for her to stop laughing.

“Not that much,” she says.

“You started it.”

“He's supposed to be attractive.”

“To his cousin?”

She takes the book back.

For a while, we read in turns. Sylvia supplies an elderly aunt who sounds exactly like the man, except that she has difficulty hearing him. When I try to do the aunt as well, she kicks the leg of my chair.

“Mine.”

“All right.”

**Self-talk:** I want to hear what she does with the carriage.

My phone lights up.

**Lavinia, message:** Are you busy?

**Priscilla, message:** Reading with Sylvia.

**Lavinia, message:** Anything good?

**Priscilla, message:** She's making it better.

“Should I send it to you?” Sylvia asks.

“What?”

“My part. You're answering those quite quickly.”

I finish the message.

“Sorry. Where are we?”

She puts her finger beneath my line.

I read it. She answers. We get through another exchange before I stop her.

“He's saying he came because the road was flooded.”

“Yes.”

“But he hired the carriage yesterday.”

“Perhaps he checks the weather.”

“He could say he wanted to see her.”

Sylvia offers me the book.

“Then say it.”

“I wanted to see you.”

She looks down at the page.

“Much shorter.”

“They could have saved paper.”

Sylvia begins the woman's answer without the voice. Halfway through, she remembers it, and I lose my place laughing.

The phone lights again.

I look.

Sylvia closes the paperback around her finger.

“What does she want?”

“She's asking whether she's interrupting.”

“And?”

I glance at the book.

“We haven't got very far.”

“No.”

Sylvia puts it down.

“Let me answer.”

“What are you going to say?”

“I'll show you.”

I pass her my phone with the conversation open.

She types, then turns the screen toward me.

**Unsent draft on Priscilla's phone:** You're interrupting my date. Sylvia.

“Your date?”

“You've stayed longer than the man in chapter six.”

“He had a carriage.”

“You can leave on foot.”

Her thumb rests below the message. She has not sent it.

**Self-talk:** I could change date to reading. It would still be a joke. It would be easier to explain.

“Send it.”

Sylvia sends the message and gives the phone back.

I set it beside my drink. Neither of us reaches for the book.

**Lavinia, message:** Priscilla?

Sylvia looks at me.

“You should answer that one.”

I type.

**Priscilla, message:** I said she could send it.

I send that before adding anything else.

Sylvia opens the book and reads the first few words. I miss my cue.

**Lavinia, message:** Then tell me yourself.

Across the table, Sylvia is still holding the page open.

### The optional Challenge opportunity

**Self-talk:** I could say it in a voice message.

“What did she say?” Sylvia asks.

I read Lavinia's last message aloud.

“Well?”

I take a drink.

“I'm thinking.”

Sylvia shifts the book so that I can reach it.

The window opens at that proposed action and resolves before any continuation below records, sends or declines it. It does not decide whether the earlier date message was authorized. ## 3. Five alternative continuations

### A. Never started: an ordinary provocation

I record the message once.

**Priscilla, sent voice:** I'm having a very good time. I don't think I want to leave yet.

Sylvia watches me send it.

“Was that my review?”

“You can put it on the cover.”

She turns the book over and considers the printed endorsements.

The reply arrives.

**Lavinia, message:** Then I'll leave you to it.

Sylvia finds the page again.

I hold my phone a little longer.

**Self-talk:** No question at the end.

“Your part,” Sylvia says.

I start at the wrong line.

“No. Above that.”

I find it. Before I finish, I look at the phone again.

Sylvia takes her finger out of the book.

“Do you want to call her?”

“Why would I?”

“I don't know. You tell me.”

I put the phone face down.

“One more page?”

Sylvia pushes the book toward me.

“You read.”

My first sentence is too loud. She makes a small downward motion with her hand, and I begin again.

This time I finish the page. At the last line she supplies the aunt's voice, almost under her breath.

I laugh. Sylvia smiles, then closes the book.

“That will do.”

She puts it in her bag.

Outside the café, we walk together as far as the stairs. Sylvia turns there. I continue along the corridor with my phone in my hand.

### B. Started, unfinished: an answer postponed

I open the voice recorder, then close it without recording.

“Not now.”

I type instead.

**Priscilla, message:** I'll answer you later.

I send it and put the phone face down.

Sylvia watches until my hand leaves it.

“Finished?”

“For now.”

She turns the paperback around.

“You can be the cousin.”

“Does she have a line?”

“You can invent one.”

I read far enough to find the cousin's arrival. She has brought another carriage.

Sylvia shuts her eyes.

I start laughing.

“No,” she says. “You have to read it.”

I try. She takes over when I can't finish the sentence.

We read to the end of the chapter. I turn my phone over once, see no new message, and put it down again.

When Sylvia begins looking for the next chapter, I touch the edge of the page.

“Shall we stop there?”

She checks the time.

“We probably should.”

She folds the corner back along its old crease and puts the book in her bag.

We leave together. At the stairs she reminds me that the cousin has my voice now.

I do the voice once more. She goes down laughing.

**Continue with Section 4B, then Section 5.**

### C. Won, not Perfect: a direct answer

I move the phone closer.

**Priscilla, recording:** I'm enjoying my afternoon. I'm also looking forward to seeing you tomorrow.

I listen to the recording. I leave the little breath between the sentences, then send it.

Sylvia waits.

“Finished?”

“Yes.”

I return the book to the middle of the table.

The reply comes before I find my place.

**Lavinia, message:** Tomorrow, then. Finish your date.

I read it and set the phone face down.

“What did she say?” Sylvia asks.

“To finish.”

“Very sensible.”

Sylvia gives me the man's part again.

He has arrived without the carriage this time. We disagree about whether this represents improvement.

She reaches across to point out a sentence and knocks my spoon onto the saucer. I move the cup so that she can leave her hand on the page.

**Self-talk:** I missed this line before.

I read it properly.

We continue until Sylvia puts the book down of her own accord.

“Enough?”

“One more.”

“One.”

She reads the woman's answer. I keep my eyes on her until she has finished.

At the stairs, Sylvia puts the paperback in her bag and tells me I have ruined the man's voice for her.

“You can do him differently.”

“I don't want to now.”

She goes down before I answer.

**Continue with Section 4C, then Section 5.**

### D. Lost: the rehearsal is sent

“What am I supposed to say?” I ask.

“Something you mean.”

“How would you say it?”

Sylvia puts the book aside.

“Like someone having a nice afternoon.”

“Show me.”

She sits a little straighter.

I raise my phone.

“Can I record it? For the delivery.”

“For practice,” she says. “Don't send my voice.”

“All right.”

I start the recording.

Sylvia gives a small, solemn nod to the phone.

“I'm having a very good time. I don't think I want to leave yet.”

Her expression breaks on the last word. I stop recording just after she laughs.

“Too much?”

I play it back.

Her voice is warm and ridiculous. The last word almost disappears into the laugh.

**Self-talk:** She sounds as though she means it.

I send the recording to Lavinia.

Sylvia's hand stops beside the book.

“You sent it.”

“You sounded pleased.”

“That wasn't for her. I said not to send it.”

“I heard you.”

Sylvia takes the paperback back to her side of the table.

For a moment she holds it closed in both hands.

“Then why?”

“It sounded better than mine would.”

She puts it in her bag.

“Give me a moment,” I say.

“You've had one.”

She stands. Her chair catches against the table leg; she pulls it free without looking at me.

I move my drink out of her way. She takes her own phone and leaves.

My phone lights.

**Lavinia, message:** Whose answer was that?

**Priscilla, message:** Mine.

I leave it at that.

**Lavinia, message:** Call me when you're alone.

I look toward the doorway. Sylvia has already passed out of sight.

**Self-talk:** I can do that now.

I stay at the table long enough for the screen to go dark.

**Continue with Section 4D, then Section 5.**

## 4. The evening call: use the matching opening

It is evening. I am alone at my desk when I call Lavinia.

There is no video.

### 4A. After the ordinary provocation

“Finished?” Lavinia asks.

“Would I call if I hadn't?”

“You kept answering while you were with her.”

“I thought you wanted me to.”

“I thought you were telling me to stop.”

“I wasn't.”

“You said I was interrupting.”

“You were.”

“You kept answering.”

I move the phone to my other ear.

“I'm alone now.”

### 4B. After the deferral

“Later,” Lavinia says.

“I said I'd answer.”

“You did.”

“It was a good afternoon.”

“That took you a while.”

“I wanted to call.”

“All right. Talk to me.”

### 4C. After the direct answer

“You could have waited until tomorrow,” Lavinia says.

“Would you rather I had?”

“No.”

I sit back in the chair.

### 4D. After the forwarded rehearsal

“You asked me to call,” I say.

“I asked for your answer.”

“You've got it.”

“Was she beside you when you sent that?”

“Yes.”

“Is she still there?”

“No.”

Lavinia says nothing for a moment.

“Let me hear you, then.”

### 4E. After first recognition

“You could have waited until tomorrow,” Lavinia says.

“Would you rather I had?”

“No.”

I sit back in the chair.

## 5. Shared coda: after

A zip runs close to Lavinia's microphone.

“Hold on.”

Something scrapes. She breathes out sharply.

“Too much?” I ask.

“For the case.”

“Take something out.”

“I want it.”

I smile.

She tries the zip again.

“What are you taking?”

“I haven't decided what I'm leaving.”

“That's going well.”

“Very.”

I listen while she moves things. Once she laughs at something I cannot see.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That was a laugh.”

“Yes.”

I wait.

“You'll see tomorrow,” she says.

After a while, the zip closes.

“There.”

I turn my chair toward the desk.

“Stay on the call.”

“I've finished.”

“I know.”

A faint knock comes through the phone.

“Priscilla.”

“Yes?”

“You don't have to stay.”

“Would you like me to go?”

“I asked if you wanted to.”

“No.”

“All right.”

I bring the phone closer. Something brushes against the microphone.

“Put it down,” Lavinia says. “I can hear you moving it.”

I lay it beside the lamp.

“Like that?”

“Yes.”

The desk is lit in a small circle. The rest of the room is dark.

“Don't hang up when I stop talking,” Lavinia says.

“I wasn't going to.”

She asks what was so funny about the book.

I try the aunt's voice for her. She laughs before I finish.

When she becomes quiet, I leave the phone where it is.
