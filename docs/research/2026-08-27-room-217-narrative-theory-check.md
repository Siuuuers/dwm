# Room 2.17 dialogue: narrative-theory check

Date: 2026-08-27
Status: non-authoritative external research. This note does not amend canon, scene text, implementation requirements, or plans.

## Question

Compare two possible Love-state endings for the Room 2.17 encounter:

**A — ordinary, explicit logistics**

> **LAVINIA:** Friday. This room.\
> **PRISCILLA:** What time?\
> **LAVINIA:** After rehearsal.

**B — highly elliptical shorthand**

> **LAVINIA:** Friday.\
> **PRISCILLA:** After rehearsal.

## Recommendation

**Keep A.** Its practical meaning is clear while its emotional meaning remains unresolved. Lavinia chooses another day and the same room; Priscilla responds by asking rather than completing the arrangement for her; Lavinia supplies the boundary. That is an intelligible action-and-reaction sequence, not an explanation of reconciliation.

B compresses the referent as well as the emotion. It can therefore look less like naturally shared knowledge and more like an authored demonstration that Priscilla can decode Lavinia. If a later scene confirms the intended referent, B becomes a riddle with a solution; that is not automatically greater interpretive plurality.

The usable synthesis is:

> **Make the practical event clear; leave its motive non-final.**

## Source-grounded reading

### Robert McKee: the words should perform the turn

McKee's official *Dialogue* page treats speech as verbal action: talk answers a need, serves a purpose, and does something. His official scene essay likewise argues that a scene should produce a perceptible change in a value-charged condition. See [*Dialogue: The Art of Verbal Action*](https://mckeestory.com/books/dialogue/) and [“Do Your Scenes Turn?”](https://mckeestory.com/do-your-scenes-turn/).

Applied here, the small turn is not “they reconcile.” It is:

```text
institutionally required collaboration
-> Lavinia personally arranges its recurrence
```

A makes that turn perceptible through three distinct verbal actions. B retains atmosphere but makes the event itself less certain: “Friday” might name a deadline, a folder, or a meeting, while Priscilla's reply supplies a time without being asked. The latter can also shift too much authorship back to Priscilla when this variation is meant to show Lavinia choosing continuity.

This does not require on-the-nose dialogue. McKee's first-party lesson on subtext warns against characters stating what lies underneath their behaviour; A never says forgiveness, intimacy, possession, or love. See [“How to Bring Subtext to the Surface Without Writing on the Nose”](https://mckeestory.com/how-to-bring-subtext-to-the-surface-without-writing-on-the-nose/) and his discussion of minimalist subtext in [*45 Years*](https://mckeestory.com/45-years-2015/).

### Alain Robbe-Grillet: retain the room and gesture before explaining them

Robbe-Grillet explicitly cautioned that *Pour un nouveau roman* was not a universal theory or a school manifesto. The publisher's author statement calls its claims provisional lines of development, not laws. See [Éditions de Minuit, *Pour un nouveau roman*](https://www.leseditionsdeminuit.fr/livre-Pour_un_nouveau_roman-2229-1-1-0-1.html).

Two narrower principles are still useful:

- In a 1965 interview, Robbe-Grillet objects to traditional psychological analysis because it labels and orders passions until it freezes them. See [Robbe-Grillet presenting *La Maison de rendez-vous*](https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1965/10/09/alain-robbe-grillet-presente-la-maison-de-rendez-vous_2194074_1819218.html).
- In a 1978 interview, he calls repetition essential to strongly structured composition while emphasizing textual movement between repeated themes. See [“Robbe-Grillet commenté par lui-même”](https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1978/09/22/robbe-grillet-commente-par-lui-meme_2987950_1819218.html).

The original publisher description of *La Jalousie* is also instructive: an unseen focal presence measures distances and gestures, and apparently innocent perceptions remain inscribed. See [Éditions de Minuit, *La Jalousie*](https://www.leseditionsdeminuit.fr/livre-La_Jalousie-1797-1-1-0-1.html).

A gives the later scene exact material to repeat with a difference: **Friday, Room 2.17, after rehearsal**. The same room can return under altered pressure without narration assigning it a psychological label. “This room” is therefore surface evidence, not an explanation of what the room symbolizes. B risks making ellipsis itself the conspicuous aesthetic object and shared comprehension its intended “secret.”

### Roland Barthes: plurality is not the same as obscuring the referent

Barthes argues against fastening a text to one author-guaranteed final meaning. Crucially, *From Work to Text* says textual plurality does **not** depend merely on ambiguous content; it arises from the relations and passages among signifiers. The university-hosted primary-text scan contains both “The Death of the Author” and “From Work to Text”: [Roland Barthes, “The Death of the Author” / “From Work to Text”](https://sites.english.unt.edu/~simpkins/Barthes%20Author%20Work.pdf). The original 1967 *Aspen* issue is catalogued in the [UbuWeb *Aspen* archive](https://ubu.punctumbooks.com/aspen/aspen5and6/).

A closes one factual question—Lavinia proposes another meeting—but does not close why she does it. A reader may still hold together anger, professional fluency, attachment, appetite for attention, control, care, and habit without being told which is the master explanation.

B can paradoxically be less plural if the writer expects one privileged decoding: “Priscilla understands Lavinia so completely that two fragments suffice.” That would replace multiple motives with a single author-designed demonstration. Barthes does not require writers to stop constructing mechanisms; he removes the author's intention as the final court of interpretation once the text is read.

### Mark Fisher: banal traces can carry the unknown

Repeater's first-party description says Fisher's weird and eerie concern the outside and unknown and are unsettling without being reducible to horror. See [Repeater Books, *The Weird and the Eerie*](https://repeaterbooks.com/audiobooks/sample-the-weird-and-the-eerie/).

More specifically, an authorized extract discusses eerie agency becoming perceptible through effects and traces, with only minimal material for speculation; it calls an ordinary simulated hotel room “unnerving in its very banality.” See [Mark Fisher, extract from *The Weird and the Eerie*](https://thequietus.com/culture/books/mark-fisher-weird-eerie-kubrick-tarkovsky-nolan-review/).

That favors A's mundane trace over B's decorative opacity. A room booking, a rehearsal schedule, and a second folder can remain fully ordinary. Their persistence and reuse can nevertheless raise questions about agency: who is extending the collaboration, who is permitting it, and when does useful procedure become a method of keeping someone? The scene need not make the logistical referent mysterious in order to keep those forces unresolved.

Fisher's hauntology should not be used as a loose synonym for recurrence. In an author interview, he links it to lost futures—possibilities that could have happened but did not. See [“They Can Be Different in the Future Too: Mark Fisher interviewed”](https://www.versobooks.com/blogs/news/3051-they-can-be-different-in-the-future-too-mark-fisher-interviewed). Friday becomes hauntological only if that promised future is later prevented, altered, or persists as the trace of a failed possibility. Merely scheduling it is not enough.

## Comparison

| Test | A: explicit logistics | B: elliptical shorthand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scene action | Lavinia's choice, Priscilla's question, and Lavinia's boundary are all legible | The referent and the division of agency are uncertain |
| Subtext | Motive remains unsaid beneath ordinary scheduling | The audience may focus on decoding the scheduling itself |
| Robbe-Grillet use | Establishes an exact room/time configuration that can recur without explanation | Risks turning opacity into an ornamental sign of intimacy |
| Barthesian plurality | One event supports several incompatible emotional readings | May reduce to one later-confirmed secret meaning |
| Fisherian atmosphere | Supplies banal traces whose later persistence can become unsettling | Withholds part of the trace; obscurity may precede any eerie question of agency |

## Limits

- None of these writers discussed this scene, these characters, or visual-novel branching. The recommendation is a synthesis, not an attributed endorsement.
- McKee offers craft heuristics, not empirical laws. A later confirmation could carry B's delayed turn if postponement were the desired design.
- Robbe-Grillet wrote across changing periods and rejected reductive labels such as “objective camera.” Precision of surface does not mean neutral perception.
- Barthes's account of authorship and textual plurality is a theory of writing and reading, not an instruction to maximize vagueness.
- Fisher analyzes aesthetic modes and cultural works; he does not supply a dialogue formula. Eeriness requires more than unexplained shorthand.
- A can still become affected if staged as a portentous declaration. It should remain flat, quick, and professionally ordinary. Its later recurrence—not emphasis in this exchange—should give it pressure.

## Decision

Retain A as the working version:

> **LAVINIA:** Friday. This room.\
> **PRISCILLA:** What time?\
> **LAVINIA:** After rehearsal.

Its facts are definite. Its relationship is not.
