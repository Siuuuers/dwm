# *La Jalousie*: Unmarked Focal Presence in a Visual Novel

**Date:** 2026-09-06  
**Status:** Research note; application accepted by the owner on 2026-09-06  
**Question:** Can the Day 6 Group presentation remain physically bound to Angela's perception while never certifying to the audience whose limited sight it occupies?

## Finding

Yes. The useful distinction is not between a known and an unknown **writer-side** anchor. It is between a rigorously fixed writer-side anchor and an **audience-facing attribution that remains unmarked**.

For Day 6, the writers may know that every selected fact is available to Angela from one exact position while the game never says `Angela looked back`, never labels a narration box as Angela's, and never later solves the viewpoint. The audience may infer Angela from geometry and timing, but inference is not certification. This is closer to the operative pleasure of *La Jalousie* than either an announced Angela POV or a free-floating omniscient camera.

This conclusion strengthens rather than relaxes the current epistemic contract: the viewpoint may be hidden from the audience, but it cannot be hidden from the writers.

## Primary-source basis

### 1. An absent grammatical subject can still be the scene's spatial centre

Éditions de Minuit's official presentation describes the narrator-husband as present from first sentence to last while having no name or face: a void among objects from which lines depart or at which they terminate. It also makes the double meaning of *jalousie*—jealousy and the slatted blind—an apparatus for seeing and being unable to see. This supports a focal presence made concrete by spatial restriction rather than by a pronoun or identity label. [Éditions de Minuit, *La Jalousie*](https://www.leseditionsdeminuit.fr/livre-La_Jalousie-2731-1-1-0-1.html)

The publisher's official opening excerpt demonstrates the method. It establishes pillars, shadows, doors, terrace, window, balustrade, and a gaze coming from the back of a room, yet it does not introduce an `I` who owns that gaze. The perceiver is registered by geometry and selection. [Official opening excerpt (PDF)](https://www.leseditionsdeminuit.fr/images/extrait_2731.pdf)

### 2. Jealousy is enacted as an arrangement of attention, not supplied as a diagnosis

In a 1965 interview, Robbe-Grillet rejects psychological labels that freeze a passion. His distinction is between declaring a character “a jealous man” and making the work communicate someone in the process of living jealousy. For this project, that argues against narration such as `Angela was jealous` and in favour of a perceptual field that keeps selecting distances, departures, answers, hands, and occlusions. [Robbe-Grillet interview, *Le Monde*, 1965](https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1965/10/09/alain-robbe-grillet-presente-la-maison-de-rendez-vous_2194074_1819218.html)

### 3. The missing centre must generate the scene rather than decorate it

Robbe-Grillet later described *La Jalousie*, *The Voyeur*, and *The Erasers* as organized around a lack, and said that structure produces meaning. The implication for the Group presentation is strict: concealing the perceptual owner is valuable only if the limits of that unowned-looking view determine what can be seen, heard, repeated, or lost. A mysterious narration style pasted over an otherwise complete scene would be imitation without function. [Robbe-Grillet interview, *Le Monde*, 1976](https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1976/01/16/alain-robbe-grillet-chez-moi-c-est-la-structure-qui-produit-le-sens_2955831_1819218.html)

### 4. Precision does not make the view neutral

Robbe-Grillet explicitly resisted the received idea that his method was objective or a merely scientific eye. Exact-looking description can be radically shaped by imagination and selection. Therefore the Day 6 view should not behave like security footage. Its measurements and omissions can be emotionally contaminated even though it never names the emotion or the perceiver. [Robbe-Grillet interview, *The Paris Review*, 1986](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/2819/the-art-of-fiction-no-91-alain-robbe-grillet)

## Adaptation rule proposed for this project

Working term: **unmarked focal presence**.

1. The passage has one writer-registered anchor. In the attended Day 6 Group presentation, that anchor remains Angela.
2. The audience-facing text and UI do not identify the anchor. No `Angela saw`, `Angela heard`, first-person tag, thought-box portrait, or later explanation is used.
3. Every visible, audible, or perceptually narrated fact must remain physically available from Angela's exact position at that instant.
4. The passage ends when her sight and hearing end. It cannot linger in the room merely because the audience wants the private consequence.
5. The selected facts may betray a pressure of attention—who reaches for a bag, who answers whom, what movement stops—but may not certify jealousy, motive, or ownership of the gaze.
6. The private Two-friends presentation supplies different pair-bound evidence; it does not reveal that Angela owned the Group view or decode what her selection meant.
7. Use this as a bounded construction, not a recurring decorative filter. Repetition is earned only when the changed detail changes the audience's evidence.

This is not an exception to `anchor-bound does not mean anchor-tagged`. It clarifies that the anchor declaration is writer-facing. Audience uncertainty does not authorize head-hopping, impossible knowledge, or an invisible narrator.

## Day 6 Group application

The strongest version is not `Angela walks away and looks back`. That sentence gives away the perceptual owner before the construction can operate. The scene can instead make Angela's departure and the pair's unfinished attachment share one narrowing field.

Non-canonical micro-audition:

> Angela: I should go.
>
> Priscilla's fingers close around the strap of her bag.
>
> Lavinia: Are you leaving too?
>
> The edge of the door divides Priscilla's wrist from her hand.
>
> The strap settles against the table again.

The important fact is not this exact wording. It is the construction:

- Angela is the lawful writer-side anchor.
- No sentence attributes the view to her.
- The door edge makes the limit of the gaze active rather than decorative.
- Lavinia's request and Priscilla's arrested departure are available, but their aftermath is not.
- The audience can suspect who saw it without receiving proof or an explanatory reveal.

The `wrist from her hand` image should survive only if the final visual blocking makes it ordinary and exact. If it looks poetically arranged for symbolism, replace it with a plainer occlusion—the door covering Priscilla's face, the bag remaining visible after her expression is lost, or Lavinia's last word audible after the room is no longer visible.

## Medium-specific danger pass

| Danger | Why it breaks the effect | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| First-person camera movement follows Angela to the door | It certifies the focal owner | Keep the presentation outwardly impersonal; let geometry, not a POV-camera flourish, define access |
| Angela's portrait or name appears on the perceptual prose box | It labels the gaze | Use the same unowned-looking prose treatment already allowed by the narrative contract |
| The room continues after Angela could no longer see or hear it | It converts a limited consciousness into an omniscient camera | Cut exactly when the available channel closes |
| A later route says that Angela looked back | It turns productive uncertainty into a clue with an answer | Let later facets complicate the event, not certify its focal ownership |
| The shot supplies both Angela's exterior position and the pair's private interior detail | It shows more than one consciousness or a detached camera knows | Choose one physically coherent field; never display both sides of the threshold as simultaneous knowledge |
| Object geometry becomes conspicuously symbolic | It makes authorial intention louder than the relationship | Retain only spatial details that control access, occlusion, timing, or evidence |

## Challenge to the absolute formulation

`The audience cannot know` should mean **the work never certifies whose limit it is**, not **no attentive player may form a strong hypothesis**. The latter would require arbitrary concealment or several interchangeable hidden observers and would weaken physical fairness. *La Jalousie* gains force because the missing perceiver can be inferred from the arrangement while never becoming an ordinary named narrator.

For this game, the desirable state is therefore:

> Writer certainty; audience suspicion; textual non-certification.

## Recommendation

Approve unmarked focal presence as the Day 6 Group method, with Angela fixed only in the writer-facing scene specification. Do not yet approve the exact door, bag, or line wording. First audition the construction against the scene's final apartment geometry and the Group/Two-friends evidence split.
