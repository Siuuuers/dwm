## 2. Dramatic Function

Lavinia has returned ordinarily after completing her sponsored company
attachment. She asks to join one of Angela's real Open Week astronomy tasks.
Angela accepts with a time rather than a declaration.

The view is sharp for Angela and not sharp for Lavinia without anything being
broken. The ordinary observer-dependent difference creates the gap. All
six outcomes complete the same physical task; they differ only in what each
woman does with attention, custody, verification, continued usefulness, and a
solved condition.

The scene must not explain what focus symbolizes. Its object remains an object.
Its smallest durable material change is one completed readiness row. Its
relationship consequence comes only from the code-owned board outcome.

The title `It Was in Focus` is private writer-facing language. The audience
does not see it as a caption or scene title.

## 4. Invitation

This exchange is the R2 solo offer itself. It is not preceded by a second Day 2
Angela–Lavinia message. Lavinia knows Angela's ordinary astronomy life and the
Saturday activity generally; Angela supplies the current-day fact and chooses
the meeting time.

**REACTION TEST — invitation; illustrative dialogue, not final DTL.**

> **LAVINIA:** Are you preparing a telescope for Saturday? \
> **ANGELA:** I’m checking one this afternoon. \
> **LAVINIA:** Let me try it. \
> **ANGELA:** Five.

Opening the solo invitation remains the mechanical acceptance action. `Five`
is illustrative authored correspondence, not a Schedule UI time or a new
time-window mechanic. Any later final-DTL mutation must preserve three facts:
Angela selects the time, the meeting remains in usable late daylight, and the
next-day line `You chose the time` remains literally true.

`Let me try it` carries two true motives without making either an alibi:
Lavinia genuinely wants the telescope and genuinely wants Angela's company.

## 5. Physical Staging

### 5.1 Location and equipment

- Use a covered astronomy preparation area at an open observing threshold.
- The telescope is already acclimated and points through open air, never
  through glass.
- Use the same low-power visual eyepiece for Angela and Lavinia.
- Do not canonize the telescope's optical design, focuser construction,
  drawtube, finder type, reticle, screws, mount, or diagnosis of either woman's
  eyesight.
- Stage ordinary daylight rather than picturesque dusk.
- The telescope points into a sector safely away from the Sun. Direct sunlight
  remains behind the covered position and outside every optical field; nobody
  pans toward it.

### 5.2 Target

The target is the point where one horizontal construction seam meets the
outside vertical corner of a distant, blank concrete stair core.

The target is fixed, windowless, unoccupied, and aggressively unimportant. It
provides a sharp edge and an exact finder reference without importing text,
privacy, a person, a roofline, a bright point, or a repeated grid. It receives
no ominous sound, close-up, picturesque emphasis, or symbolic interpretation.

No audience-visible prose is required merely to restate the target. If later
surface authorship names it, the prose may report only its observable geometry
and must not state why it matters.

### 5.3 Readiness record and next task

The two relevant rows are:

```text
LOW-POWER EYEPIECE — USABLE FOCUS CONFIRMED
FINDER-TO-MAIN ALIGNMENT — PENDING
```

The first row confirms that usable focus can be reached. It does not claim one
permanent or observer-independent focus setting. After it is marked, Angela
still must refocus for her own eye, centre the seam/corner reference in the
main field, and verify the finder against that reference. This makes `I haven’t
finished` physically and procedurally true.

## 6. Governing Optical Law

The unchanged eyepiece setting may be sharp for Angela and blurred for Lavinia.
Ordinary differences in observer eyesight and accommodation can require
different focus positions. The scene does not diagnose either woman, specify
glasses or contacts, or use uncorrected astigmatism as a hidden explanation.

Turning through best focus, continuing until the image worsens, reversing, and
stopping is an ordinary focusing method. Angela can observe Lavinia's hand
pause, continue, reverse, and stop. Angela cannot see Lavinia's optical image,
know the exact instant it became sharp, or infer a microscopic mechanical
coordinate from Lavinia's report.

Governing knowledge sentence:

> Angela can foresee Lavinia's method from visible evidence; she cannot see
> Lavinia's focus.

The released scene never displays or describes Lavinia's private optical image.

## 7. Common Attended Hinge

Angela obtains a sharp view through the low-power eyepiece and steps aside.
Lavinia looks through the unchanged eyepiece without touching the focuser.

**REACTION TEST — common hinge; illustrative dialogue, not final DTL.**

> **LAVINIA:** It’s not in focus. \
> **ANGELA:** It was. \
> **LAVINIA:** For you. \
> **ANGELA:** Yes.

Lavinia places her fingers on the focus knob but does not turn it. The solo
Minesweeper challenge begins.

Do not mark readiness before the board. After the board, every branch obtains
real usable focus and uses the invariant report `It’s sharp`. Synonyms are not
introduced by outcome, because the physical result is the same.

## 9. Relationship-Outcome Branches

### 9.1 Hatred

The relational exchange occurs before Lavinia focuses.

**REACTION TEST — Hatred; illustrative dialogue, not final DTL.**

> **LAVINIA:** You could have done this alone. \
> **ANGELA:** I could. \
> **LAVINIA:** But you told me when. \
> **ANGELA:** You asked.

Lavinia adjusts the focus alone, reverses once, and reports:

> **LAVINIA:** It’s sharp.

Angela marks `LOW-POWER EYEPIECE — USABLE FOCUS CONFIRMED`. End without a
farewell, repair, punishment, or explanatory interpretation.

**Turn:** Angela treats accepting Lavinia as something Lavinia authored alone.

### 9.2 Upset

Lavinia adjusts and reports `It’s sharp`. Angela marks the completed row,
retakes the eyepiece, and ordinarily refocuses for her own eye so she can
continue finder alignment.

**REACTION TEST — Upset; illustrative dialogue, not final DTL.**

> **LAVINIA:** You changed it back. \
> **ANGELA:** I haven’t finished. \
> **LAVINIA:** I know.

Angela reaches for the finder. Cut before Lavinia either departs or settles.
Add no footsteps, farewell, held shared silence, reaction close-up, or final
focuser image.

**Turn:** Lavinia's valid contribution is accepted and recorded, then ceases to
be needed for the legitimate continuing task.

### 9.3 Amused

Lavinia adjusts, reports `It’s sharp`, and Angela marks the completed row.
Angela then looks once through Lavinia's setting without touching the focuser.

**REACTION TEST — Amused; illustrative dialogue, not final DTL.**

> **ANGELA:** You made it worse. \
> **LAVINIA:** For you. \
> **ANGELA:** Yes.

Stop. The repeated `For you / Yes` reverses the common hinge exactly once. Add
no laugh, smile cue, telescope personification, correction, or explanation.

**Turn:** Angela voluntarily tests Lavinia's incompatible setting and accepts
being the joke's losing observer.

### 9.4 Loved

Lavinia adjusts, reports `It’s sharp`, and Angela marks the completed row. The
focus task has ended.

**REACTION TEST — Loved; illustrative dialogue, not final DTL.**

> **LAVINIA:** Do you want me to stay? \
> **ANGELA:** Yes.

Angela begins the next ordinary checklist task. Lavinia remains. Add no
gratitude, romantic summary, relationship label, or prose explaining that
Lavinia is no longer useful. Any later refocus occurs only because the next
task requires it and receives no emphasis.

**Turn:** Lavinia asks for company after the instrument no longer needs her;
Angela owns one bounded preference.

### 9.5 Foresight

Lavinia turns the focus knob, pauses, continues in the same direction,
reverses, and stops. Do not state that the first pause was optically sharp.

**REACTION TEST — Foresight; illustrative dialogue, not final DTL.**

> **LAVINIA:** It’s sharp. \
> **ANGELA:** You stopped, then kept turning. \
> **LAVINIA:** You didn’t say anything. \
> **ANGELA:** You hadn’t finished.

Angela marks the completed row.

**Turn:** Angela notices the moment at which she could intervene and waits for
Lavinia to finish her own method.

### 9.6 Dark

Lavinia first obtains real focus and reports `It’s sharp`. Angela marks the
completed row. Only after the record is true does Lavinia, without leaving the
eyepiece, turn away from the accepted setting, remove her hand, and continue
looking.

**REACTION TEST — Dark; illustrative dialogue, not final DTL.**

> **LAVINIA:** Now it isn’t. \
> **ANGELA:** You moved it. \
> **LAVINIA:** Yes.

Only after Lavinia's admission does Angela take the knob.

> **ANGELA:** Tell me when.

Angela turns it.

> **LAVINIA:** There.

Angela stops. Lavinia continues looking. Add no touch emphasis, gaze exchange,
breath cue, smile, `slowly`, eroticized proximity, metaphor, or interpretive
closure.

**Turn:** Lavinia makes a solved, harmless physical problem ask for renewed
attention; Angela recognizes the test and rewards it because it permits action
without requiring either woman to name a preference.

The first Dark point must remain quiet. The act is reversible and physically
minor; the learned relational pattern is the danger. Do not stage it as a
miniature Totally Dark ending.

## 13. Rejected and Retired Mutations

The following alternatives do not survive this audition:

- `Let me look before you call it ready` — foretells the later dispute and makes
  the author visible;
- `Seven` — requires darkness or a new illuminated-target assumption and breaks
  the approved daylight alignment task;
- the earlier provisional `Four thirty` — physically lawful but retired in
  favor of the plainer `Five`, which permits more arrival and rest time;
- a roofline — risks silently consuming `The Bright Point`;
- a ventilation louvre — repeated slats rhyme too neatly with the board grid;
- a small pipe-support bracket — visually and mechanically fussier than needed;
- a telescope type, indexed drawtube, exact repeated focus coordinate, or
  astigmatism explanation — unsupported and unnecessary;
- showing Lavinia leave in Upset — drifts toward Hatred;
- showing Lavinia settle in Upset — drifts toward Loved;
- a silent symbolic focus reset — asks the knob to carry the entire emotional
  turn;
- permission or warning before Angela resumes work in Upset — repairs the wound
  too quickly;
- `Did you change your mind?` in the missed form — explains Lavinia's fear too
  nakedly this early;
- `Do you want me there?` after the Hospital explanation — creates another
  relational event and spends later directness;
- `I would have come`, `acceptable excuse`, or complaint that Angela failed to
  message — cliché, trivialization, or unsupported entitlement during danger;
- a guaranteed Bright Point echo — mechanically and dramatically forced; and
- an audience-visible scene title or thematic closing line — over-authors the
  object.
