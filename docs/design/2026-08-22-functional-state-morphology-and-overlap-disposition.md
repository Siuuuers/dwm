# Functional-State Morphology and Legal-Overlap Disposition

## 1. Accepted decision and objective

The project owner selected every recommended **A** in two current-v1 design
batches on 2026-08-22. The first batch settled the shared treatment of Hover,
Press, Disabled, Unavailable, Busy, Error, Success, Information, Unread, and
compatible-state layering. The second batch settled the exact fact-family and
legal-overlap model needed to apply those treatments without changing any
application's behavior.

This disposition records the conversationally approved conceptual functional-
state grammar in owner-approved written bytes that must exist before the
sixteen theme/accessibility tuples can be calibrated. It governs only how
already-authorized operational facts remain visibly and assistively
distinguishable. It adds no player command, narrative fact, mechanic,
application, status message, story interpretation, or runtime state.

The central law is:

> Type every fact by its exact semantic target and fact family; compose every
> compatible fact; then apply the owning application's focus-order and input-
> admission law separately.

The audience never sees an "Orthogonal Fact Ledger," state matrix, diagnostic
overlay, or explanation of this system. They see only ordinary truthful UI.

## 2. Authority boundary and narrow supersession

The accepted Filed Semantic Ledger direction remains the parent visual grammar.
Its sixteen-tuple law, primitive-to-semantic-to-component dependency, invariant
Selected/Focus/Warning/Danger/Destructive/Unavailable evidence, friend-identity
law, application custody, and authored-art recolouring exclusion all remain in
force.

The parent written artifact remains separately pending owner review. This
disposition neither edits it nor marks its written bytes approved.

This disposition narrowly supersedes only three ambiguous or explicitly open
parts of that parent direction:

1. Focused, Hovered, and Pressed are no longer treated as mutually exclusive
   values of one navigation axis. They are independent focus, pointer-
   inspection, and physical-contact facts.
2. `Unavailable` is no longer collapsed into actionability. Availability or
   capability and actionability are typed separately, so unavailable content
   can remain present and inspectable while a child mutation action is
   disabled or absent. The parent's generic literal `Unavailable` label,
   dashed boundary, and hatch apply only where the application's public
   capability value is itself generic Unavailable. Accepted app-specific
   values such as `Sold out`, incompatible, or no-compatible-voice retain their
   own literal copy and registered redundant morphology without duplicate
   generic marks.
3. The previously pending morphology and legal-overlap work for Hover, Press,
   Disabled, Busy, Error, Success, Information, and Unread is completed at the
   conceptual level defined here.

Every application authority continues to decide:

- whether a fact exists and which semantic target owns it;
- visible localized copy and assistive names;
- whether a container is inspectable or focusable;
- action legality, command admission, confirmation, and recovery;
- transaction and execution custody;
- status lifetime and replacement boundaries; and
- domain-specific content, metadata, and focus restoration.

The global grammar never manufactures a fact merely because a component has a
visual token for it. When an application authority forbids a combination, this
disposition cannot make it legal.

## 3. Normalized private fact families

One projected semantic target may carry facts from the following independent
families:

| Fact family | Private values or ownership | Composition law |
|---|---|---|
| Projection | present or absent | Presence is the gate for every projected fact. |
| Content / occupancy | app-owned values such as Empty, readable, filled, or fallback-loadable | At most one app-defined content value for the same content owner. |
| Availability / capability | app-owned values such as available, unavailable, sold out, or incompatible | At most one value per exact capability owner; separate from whether an action can currently accept input. |
| Actionability | enabled or disabled | One value per exact action target. |
| Commitment / location | selected, current, or neither | One value per exact semantic target. |
| Focus ownership | focused or not focused | Independent from pointer inspection and physical contact. |
| Pointer inspection | hovered or not hovered | Lawful pointer-presence or affordance feedback on a container or action with an app-authorized inspection/help role, independent of actionability; it publishes no extra information beyond that approved help. |
| Physical contact | pressed or not pressed | Temporary input contact, never transaction success. |
| Risk / action scope | warning, danger, destructive action, or none | At most one risk value per exact semantic target. |
| Execution custody | idle, busy, temporarily inert, recovering, or terminal-locked | One value per custody owner. |
| Operational status | none, information, success, or error | One current status per exact local status owner. |
| Public metadata | identity, unread, and other explicitly disclosed app facts | Cumulative when the app owns compatible facts. |

`Unavailable` is a public availability or capability fact, not a synonym for
Disabled. Disabled means that one real action does not currently accept input.
An unavailable record, voice, clock, or source may remain present, labelled,
selected, and inspectable while each associated action is independently
enabled, disabled, or absent under its application authority.

An absent component owns no hit target, focus, hover, press, selection,
current-state projection, operational status, or assistive node. Quiet ruled
capacity, negative space, and decorative geometry are not hidden components and
receive no component state tokens.

## 4. Target typing and compound-component scope

Every fact attaches to the smallest semantic target that truthfully owns it.
Compound surfaces do not inherit one dominant state from a child, and children
do not inherit every state from a parent.

For example, an Unavailable Backup record may be:

- present and occupied at the drawer-container target;
- unavailable as a record capability;
- selected and focusable for truthful inspection;
- paired with a disabled Load action; and
- paired with a separately enabled Delete or overwrite action where Backup law
  permits it.

The whole drawer therefore does not become a disabled no-op merely because
ordinary Load is forbidden. Conversely, selecting the drawer does not make its
Load action legal.

A parent surface that is busy or recovering owns that custody fact. Only child
actions that conflict with the accepted operation become temporarily inert.
Readable, scrollable, or inspectable children remain available only where the
application explicitly permits them. No child is styled Busy merely because an
ancestor owns the transaction.

Physical pointer intersection is not automatically semantic Hover. Hover exists
only when the exact target retains a lawful pointer-inspection role in the
current custody state.

## 5. Completed shared morphology

These treatments are functional evidence. Their exact final pixel dimensions,
colours, and tuple values remain later calibration work.

### 5.1 Hover

Hover keeps the target's shape and position unchanged and uses only the owning
application's quiet local surface response. It never receives the detached
Focus outline, a badge, a lasting mark, or a new semantic label.

Hover confirms lawful pointer inspection only. It never selects, focuses,
reads, confirms, advances, purchases, admits a command, or creates canonical,
narrative, or transactional evidence. It may reveal already-authorized
operational help owned by the application; it never invents that help. Touch,
keyboard, controller, and assistive operation never depend on Hover alone.

### 5.2 Press

Press uses one brief inward or reversed contact seam inside the exact target.
It does not borrow Selected fill, Focus morphology, a success mark, scale,
bounce, or layout movement.

Press reports physical contact, not committed outcome. A committed visual state
appears only after the governing command is accepted and its publication law
passes.

When Hover and Press facts coexist, Press owns the shared contact-plane
treatment for the physical hold. Hover remains a pointer-inspection fact and
resumes its quiet visual response on release if inspection is still lawful.

An action that is already Disabled, unavailable for its required capability,
Busy-locked, temporarily inert, recovering, or terminal-locked cannot become
semantically Pressed. If custody changes during a physical hold, contact is
cancelled, held input is cleared, and a fresh activation is required.

### 5.3 Disabled

Disabled remains fully readable inside its ordinary component boundary. It uses
one consistent blocked-action bar or closed action-edge treatment. An app-
authorized literal operational reason appears only where that authority
requires one; this grammar never invents a cause, exposes a withheld mechanic,
or adds explanatory copy.

Disabled never relies on tint or opacity alone and never accepts Press or
command input. Its application authority decides whether the exact Disabled
action leaves focus order or remains focusable solely to expose already-
authorized operational help. Focus never makes it actionable. A surrounding
inspectable container remains a separate target. Assistive output may repeat
only the same approved public reason.

### 5.4 Unavailable

Unavailable retains the parent grammar's literal localized `Unavailable`
label, dashed boundary, and stable hatch or equivalent pattern where that
generic label is the application-owned public copy. A more precise accepted
projection, such as an unavailable clock token with its public semantic label
or a factual no-compatible-voice message, satisfies the literal-copy duty and
retains its application-owned redundant morphology. It receives no duplicate
`Unavailable` label, generic dash, or generic hatch from this grammar. Every
treatment attaches only to the unavailable content or capability; it does not
automatically reclassify the whole compound surface.

Unavailable content remains readable and may remain selected or inspectable
where the application permits. Its prohibited action remains independently
Disabled or absent. The hatch never obscures copy, focus, risk, identity, or
other compatible facts.

### 5.5 Busy

Busy appears only when a real accepted operation remains in progress across
visible frames. Immediate operations publish their result directly and show no
Busy interstitial.

Where an application authority exposes pending copy, it uses the exact local
present-tense operation, such as `Saving…` or `Loading…`, plus one static custody
seam. There is no generic spinner, fake percentage, animated disk, staged
progress claim, or invented minimum duration. An application authority may
instead own a silent truthful hold; this grammar does not add copy to it.

Selection, Current, visible content, public metadata, and applicable risk remain
legible. Only conflicting actions become inert. The whole application is not
dimmed or frozen when lawful inspection remains available.

### 5.6 Error

Error binds literal localized nonfiction copy and one clean status boundary or
edge to the exact failed item or operation. The last trustworthy value, record,
or surface remains visible unless the governing recovery authority proves that
it cannot be shown safely.

Error never corrupts geometry, impersonates a story anomaly, attributes failure
to Angela or another character, borrows Danger or Destructive morphology, or
replaces the application with a generic failure page unless the trusted
recovery owner truly owns the whole surface.

### 5.7 Success

The proven changed value, metadata, destination, or committed state is the
primary success evidence. Where an existing application authority already
projects Success on an existing value or status surface, that surface may use
the restrained settled-seam treatment. This grammar never creates a separate
mark, plane, label, or lifetime. Literal result copy such as `Saved` appears
only where an existing application authority already requires it and follows
that authority's lifetime.

There is no universal success toast, checkmark, celebration, reward motion, or
global timer. An operation whose durable result is already self-evident need
not manufacture a Success status.

### 5.8 Information

Where an application authority already exposes an Information or note surface
and its app-owned literal copy, this grammar may style that existing surface as
quiet, locally attached, and ordinarily single-boundary. It creates no plane,
label, fact, or lifetime; borrows no Focus, Warning, Danger, Destructive, Error,
or Busy mark; and becomes no prominent global banner unless separately
authorized by an application authority.

Information communicates only an owned operational fact. It never explains a
narrative event, identifies an undisclosed speaker, interprets evidence, hints
at a route, or tells the audience what an anomaly means.

### 5.9 Unread

Unread or `new` is app-owned public metadata. Its application authority retains
its exact morphology, copy, lifetime, and read/witness boundary. The shared law
only keeps that metadata structurally distinct from Focus, selection, identity,
risk, relationship state, route, mood, and hidden story evidence.

Contacts message-Unread specifically uses the accepted one-or-many-identical
binary mark in one fixed metadata gutter. It remains separate from friend-
identity rails and portrait treatment. There is no count, pulse, message-type
icon, or route decoration. Only the owning Contacts read boundary clears this
message-Unread fact.

Assistive projection names the same localized public Unread or `new` fact that
the owning application exposes. Focus, Hover, selection, notification
dismissal, and visual exposure do not clear it unless that application's
accepted authority explicitly defines such a public witness boundary.

## 6. Registered visual zones and stacking order

Compatible facts occupy stable visual zones rather than competing for one
dominant skin:

1. **Semantic body:** app-owned content, literal labels, identity, and domain
   marks.
2. **Commitment plane:** Selected or Current fill and filing seam.
3. **Metadata gutter:** Unread and other explicitly disclosed metadata.
4. **Availability/action edge:** the app-owned redundant availability/
   capability treatment—including generic Unavailable hatch/dash only where
   authorized—or Disabled blocked-action treatment, bound to its exact target.
5. **Risk edge:** the one Warning, Danger, or Destructive treatment owned by the
   exact component.
6. **Custody/status attachment:** an existing app-owned Busy or operational-
   status surface and its authorized copy, styled without creating a new plane.
7. **Contact plane:** quiet Hover response or inward Press contact inside the
   target.
8. **Focus plane:** the detached double outline, outermost and unclipped.

These are semantic zones, not a required scene-tree, shader stack, or set of
extra child controls. Their purpose is to prevent one fact from covering
another. Hover or Press never erases Selected, Unread, Risk, or Focus evidence;
Disabled or Busy never erases identity or truthful content; Focus never becomes
selection.

Adding, removing, or composing a morphology mark never moves component geometry,
changes hit-target bounds, or rewrites the focus-neighbour graph. App-owned
state may still truthfully change whether a target participates.

## 7. Legal-overlap matrix

### 7.1 Structurally compatible when the application owns both facts

Compatibility is evaluated first on one exact semantic target, then across the
typed targets of a compound component. A parent fact never leaks into a child,
and a child fact never reclassifies its parent.

On one exact target, the following combinations remain simultaneously legible
when that target owns both facts:

- Empty or other content with Selected or Current;
- Disabled with Selected, Current, or a truthful Risk scope;
- Selected or Current with Focus, Hover, operational status, or public
  metadata where the application owns those facts;
- Selected or Current with Press only when the exact contacted action is
  available, Enabled, and outside conflicting custody;
- Focus with Hover or Press;
- Focus with Warning, Danger, or Destructive scope;
- identity and Unread with selection, focus, lawful inspection, or custody; and
- one operational status with selection, current, focus, content, or metadata.

Across typed parent and child targets, a Selected or Current item may remain
inside a Busy, inert, modal, or recovering owner without inheriting that
owner's custody mark. A selected target is itself Busy only when the application
explicitly makes that exact target the custody owner. Child actionability and
contact remain separately typed.

Examples include an Empty selected Backup drawer, launcher Home remaining
Current while Disabled, a Selected and Focused mode, and a Contacts row
retaining identity, Unread, Selected, and Focus simultaneously.

### 7.2 Application-owned combinations

The application authority decides:

- which actions belong to each app-owned content or capability state, including
  Empty, fallback-loadable, Unavailable, sold out, or incompatible;
- whether a visible container retains a lawful focus or inspection role;
- whether Busy retains read-only focus or transfers focus to a custody owner;
- which descendants conflict with a transaction and become inert;
- whether a real Risk fact belongs to a component carrying public metadata;
- whether a status is projected, what literal copy it uses, and which factual
  boundary replaces it; and
- whether terminal content remains inspectable after its mutation actions end.

The global matrix records those decisions; it never creates them.

### 7.3 Never legal on the same semantic target

The following combinations are contradictions:

- Absent with any projected content, hit target, component state, or assistive
  node;
- two mutually exclusive values in the same closed family;
- multiple Information/Success/Error values under one local status owner;
- multiple Warning/Danger/Destructive values on one component;
- semantic Press on an action that is Disabled, lacks its required available
  capability, or is Busy-locked, inert, recovering, or terminal-locked;
- semantic Hover where no lawful inspection role exists;
- active Focus on an exact Disabled action without an app-authorized help or
  inspection role, on any exact temporarily inert or Busy-locked action, or on
  an inert background subtree;
- a lower surface retaining active interaction while a modal or recovery owner
  owns the input boundary; or
- identity or Unread being reinterpreted as Warning, Danger, relationship,
  route, mood, anomaly, or story evidence.

An unavailable record container may still accept a separate inspection command
when that command is enabled. This does not violate the Press prohibition: the
record's ordinary-use capability and its inspection action are different typed
targets.

### 7.4 Operational-status and custody transitions

For the same bound operation, Busy ends before Success or Error publishes.
Success and Error are mutually exclusive. The UI never presents `Saving…` and
`Saved`, or another pending/result pair, as simultaneous facts for one owner.

Merely offering or focusing Retry does not erase the truthful Error. Admission
of Retry or another new bound operation replaces or clears the prior local
result at the application-owned boundary; a resulting visible-duration
operation may then become Busy. Error may coexist with Recovering only when
separate exact targets explicitly own and publicly project both facts.
Information may coexist with custody only under the same target-typing rule.

## 8. Focus, input, and custody arbitration

When custody continues to project an active or readable focus target, the
owning application chooses exactly one lawful focus strategy:

1. **Retain read-only focus:** focus remains on a still-present, meaningful,
   nonconflicting inspection or scroll target; or
2. **Transfer to custody owner:** focus moves to the modal, recovery surface, or
   other exact active subtree under that authority's initial-focus law.

Busy never preserves focus on a conflicting mutation action. A Disabled action
remains focusable only where its application authority explicitly owns a
public help or inspection role; it still cannot Press or act. If the old target
becomes absent, nonfocusable, or covered by an exclusive modal boundary, focus
moves to the application's exact fallback. A stored restoration anchor is not
active focus, and invisible geometry never owns a ghost outline.

An app-authorized noninteractive transition or neutral matte is the narrow
focusless exception. It owns no focus, accepts or queues no input, and lets its
destination establish fresh focus atomically. The ordinary Ending matte
retains exactly that law.

A modal or recovery surface does not mutate, reclassify, or delete background
identity, Unread, Selected, Current, content, or risk. Where the governing
authority leaves the lower projection visible, those facts remain legible. An
application-authorized modal or Pause treatment may dim, freeze, veil, or fully
obscure that lower projection, including opaque High Contrast treatment,
without changing its underlying facts. Background controls are nevertheless
inert and absent from the active focus and assistive-action traversal. Only the
custody owner accepts input. Dismissal restores the exact lower facts and focus
according to the governing application law.

A transition into Disabled, Busy-locked, inert, recovering, terminal-locked,
modal, suspended, or replacement custody cancels any Press that can no longer
be admitted and clears held or queued physical input. A fresh activation is
required after the new owner becomes ready.

When a target becomes absent, inert, modal-covered, recovering, or otherwise
loses its inspection role, its semantic Hover clears immediately. Raw pointer
intersection cannot preserve ghost Hover; a fresh lawful pointer-inspection
boundary is required after custody returns.

## 9. Required current-v1 examples

The later component and tuple work must preserve at least these representative
compositions:

| Surface | Required simultaneous facts |
|---|---|
| Backup Empty drawer | present + Empty + Selected; its Load/Delete actionability remains separately app-owned |
| Backup Unavailable drawer | present + occupied + Unavailable + Selected and inspectable where allowed; Load separately Disabled |
| Backup visible-duration operation | selected record and readable metadata retained + exact Busy custody; only conflicting actions inert |
| Desktop launcher Home | Current + Disabled + nonfocusable |
| Contacts friend row | identity + binary Unread + Selected + Focus, when all four facts exist |
| Minesweeper mode | Selected remains distinct from Focus, Hover, Press, and Disabled |
| Trusted warning/confirmation | dialog owns Warning where applicable; its exact destructive action separately owns Destructive scope |
| Modal or recovery boundary | background public facts remain visually truthful; custody owner alone owns active focus/action traversal |
| Hospital/Ending completion hold | its retained authority may remove the rail from focus and expose no Busy/status copy; this grammar adds none |

These examples do not expand application behavior. They are collision fixtures
for the grammar that the applications already own.

## 10. Accessibility and semantic parity

Every functional fact uses redundant non-colour evidence through literal text,
shape, boundary, line, position, pattern, or an accepted combination. Colour,
motion, texture, opacity, hover, pointer presence, or sound never owns meaning
alone.

No glyph, seam, hatch, rule, pattern, or other visible mark may form a private
pictogram language or own an operational fact alone. It accompanies approved
literal copy or a standard publicly legible control convention. Assistive text
cannot make an otherwise unexplained visible sign meaningful.

Visible and assistive projections expose the same public operational fact and
no hidden narrative meaning. Assistive output may name Disabled, Unavailable,
Unread, selected/current, risk, operational status, or execution custody only
where the application visibly exposes that same public fact. A visually silent
truthful hold remains semantically silent; affected controls expose only their
ordinary public enabled, Disabled, or Unavailable state. Assistive output never
exposes internal axes, component IDs, route consequences, relationship state,
Observer data, or an explanation of scene evidence.

Reduced Motion publishes the same final morphology statically. High Contrast
and every colour-differentiation tuple preserve every required zone and
overlap. Focus remains detached and visible. Unavailable remains readable.
Modal background controls leave active assistive traversal exactly as their
trusted prompt or recovery authority requires.

## 11. Rejected alternatives and rationale

The following approaches are rejected for current v1:

- one dominant state skin that erases compatible facts;
- one navigation winner that lets pointer Hover erase keyboard Focus;
- treating a compound surface as wholly Disabled because one child action is
  unavailable;
- giving an already blocked action Press feedback;
- interpreting raw pointer overlap as a semantic inspection state;
- clearing all focus or freezing an entire application for every Busy owner;
- folding Information, Success, and Error into Warning or Busy;
- stacking several risk glyphs and edges on one target;
- dimming, freezing, veiling, or obscuring a background in a way that mutates,
  reclassifies, or replaces its underlying facts; app-authorized modal and
  Pause treatment may partially or fully obscure them under its retained law;
- universal spinners, success popups, checkmarks, or timed celebration; and
- graphical signs that explain narrative meaning or teach anomaly
  interpretation.

These alternatives either conflate distinct facts, fabricate meaning, lose
orientation, or make operational truth depend on one dominant visual effect.

## 12. Deliberately pending and excluded work

This disposition does not lock or authorize:

- raw hexadecimal values or final primitive derivatives;
- After-Hours or Midnight semantic-role mappings;
- Standard, High Contrast, Protan, Deutan, or Tritan assignments;
- any of the sixteen complete theme/accessibility tuples;
- final pixel geometry, spacing, line weight, hatch density, or exported marks;
- caption/History material calibration;
- new application states, copy, status lifetimes, actions, or confirmations;
- audio, TTS, ambience, or interaction sound;
- Observer mechanics, anomaly design, avatar/photo-frame work, richer scene
  apertures, special-ending reconciliation, or any other deferred feature;
- Godot scenes, resources, scripts, shaders, assets, imports, tests, or runtime
  compatibility work; or
- an implementation plan.

The next visual design gate may calibrate the sixteen tuples only after this
written disposition is separately approved. That later gate must consume this
grammar without reopening application behavior or inventing missing facts.

## 13. Later verification obligations

Any future implementation authority must prove at least:

- every Section 9 collision fixture across all effective tuples;
- Focus + Hover, Focus + Press, Selected + Focus, Current + Disabled, and
  identity + Unread + Selected + Focus without occlusion;
- Focus + Hover + Press resolving Press on the contact plane during the hold and
  restoring Hover after release only when the same target remains present and
  lawfully hoverable; otherwise Hover clears;
- zero Press on exact Disabled, unavailable-capability, Busy-locked, inert,
  recovering, or terminal-locked actions, including mid-hold custody change;
- app-specific Disabled focus law, including nonfocusable Backup/Home actions
  and focusable Narrative help where authorized, with neither accepting Press;
- zero active Focus on exact temporarily inert or Busy-locked actions and on
  inert/modal-covered background subtrees, while a separately inspectable
  container remains legal where owned;
- Unavailable content remaining inspectable where allowed while its prohibited
  mutation stays Disabled and non-pressable;
- immediate operations producing no Busy state and visible-duration operations
  never claiming fake progress;
- Busy ending before same-owner Success/Error, Success/Error mutual exclusion,
  Retry admission replacing the prior result, and separately typed
  Recovering+Error cases;
- Error preserving the last trustworthy state, Success publishing only after
  proof, and Information never borrowing risk or custody morphology;
- Contacts one/many-identical message-Unread, its Contacts-only read boundary,
  and other app-owned Unread/`new` morphology and lifetime;
- custody transitions cancelling invalid Press and clearing held input;
- both lawful focus strategies, exact fallback, modal focus trapping, and
  restoration without ghost focus;
- modal/Pause background facts remaining present and legible where their
  authority leaves the lower projection visible; when it obscures or hides the
  lower projection, the facts remain unmutated, absent from active traversal,
  and exactly restorable without a fabricated replacement;
- one risk value per component and separate dialog/action risk ownership;
- one local operational status at a time with app-owned replacement;
- modality-appropriate keyboard, pointer, touch, controller, and supported
  assistive parity, without synthesizing Hover for non-pointer input;
- non-colour, Reduced Motion, native-pixel, localization, and text-size proofs;
  and
- zero narrative, relationship, route, anomaly, or hidden-state meaning from
  functional marks.

Those are future acceptance obligations, not claims about the present runtime.
