# Narrative Constitution Ablation Pass 01

## 1. Outcome and authority

This record preserves the first adversarial ablation of the working Narrative
Constitution. It does not silently amend owner-confirmed canon. It separates
real defects from deliberate trade-offs and reviewer objections caused by an
intentionally incomplete contract.

The single-model review is complete. A cross-model second opinion was offered
and explicitly skipped by the owner. The reconciliation therefore proceeds
with single-model findings only.

The review found four kinds of result:

- direct contradictions or over-broad rules that should be repaired;
- deliberate costs that remain acceptable if their safeguards survive;
- questions already labelled open in the working ledger; and
- objections that disappear once omitted owner context is restored.

This audit record never authorizes implementation. Following the owner's
2026-09-03 approval, its applicable repairs were folded into the working ledger
and runtime-behavior record. No authoritative Bible, final plot, Dialogic
timeline, or runtime code was changed.

## 2. Claim under test

**Claim:** Ablation should be a clustered quality gate rather than a ritual
performed after every micro-decision.

**Why it matters:** Immediate review of every sentence would interrupt creative
causality, repeatedly test rules before their neighbours exist, and make the
project optimize for defensibility instead of dramatic life. Waiting until the
whole seven-day plot is written would make foundational repair expensive.

## 3. Recommended ablation cadence

**OWNER-CONFIRMED 2026-09-03**

Use three decision classes:

1. **Immediate gate:** ablate a decision as soon as it changes agency,
   causality, observable surfaces, consent, Hospitalization, progression,
   persistence, or ending reachability.
2. **Cluster gate:** ablate a coherent group of scene/style decisions when its
   manual section closes. Normally this means three to five coupled rulings,
   not every wording choice.
3. **Milestone gate:** ablate the complete Day 1 causal design, then the full
   Day 1–7 spine, before dialogue drafting treats either as settled.

Pure wording, examples, labels, and formatting wait for the relevant cluster
gate unless they accidentally change story law. Unchanged material is not sent
through repeated fresh-context cycles. Each artifact receives at most three
cycles before unresolved disagreement returns to the owner.

Every gate asks seven compact questions:

- **Removal:** What necessary dramatic or mechanical work disappears if this
  rule is removed?
- **Simplification:** Can a weaker or smaller rule do the same work?
- **Collision:** Does it contradict another confirmed law or open decision?
- **Variation:** Can two meaningfully different scenes obey it without falling
  into the same rhythm?
- **Evidence:** Does it confuse physical fact, character knowledge, audience
  inference, or private author truth?
- **Maintenance:** Does it create state, branches, assets, or exceptions that
  later material never consumes?
- **Agency:** Does it manufacture desire, consent, incapacity, guilt, or another
  character's decision?

## 4. Reconciliation key

- **Contract misread:** the objection depends on context deliberately absent
  from the review extract or attacks an item already marked open.
- **Valid + actionable:** the text contains a real defect; amend it after the
  connected owner ruling is clear.
- **Valid trade-off:** the cost is real, but a confirmed purpose presently
  outweighs it; retain the choice and its safeguard.
- **Noise:** the objection does not survive comparison with the actual text.

## 5. Relationship and board mechanics

### RM-01 — “Priscilla and Lavinia's relationship cannot change”

**Classification: Valid + actionable at proposal level; resolved 2026-09-03.**
An immutable relationship is not distinguishable if conduct, trust, distance,
protection, harm, disclosure, and chosen future may all change. The owner
clarified that the game freezes one of four state/tone combinations at the
start of each run. Audience operations cannot mutate that categorical form,
while the women's own actions may still change concrete conduct and residue
inside it.

### RM-02 — Local-continuation contradiction in Section 7.2

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** Section 7.2 says only an ordinary reply
or solo board selects a local authored continuation, while Section 7.4 says a
visible pair board selects one too. It also calls the pair law reopened after
the ledger records it as owner-confirmed. Amend Section 7.2 to name all three
selectors and distinguish the consequence each is permitted to select.

### RM-03 — Pair board when Angela is absent

**Classification: Valid trade-off.** Allowing an audience-operated board to
select a Priscilla–Lavinia conduct continuation gives the audience causal power
over which authored possibility becomes the run's fact. That is a deliberate
cost of the confirmed pair-board direction. Preserve it only if the text stays
clear that the board selects a complete counterfactual continuation; their
history, reasons, feelings, and consent still arise from the characters rather
than the board.

### RM-04 — Global meaning assigned to Perfect, Solved, and Exploded

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** Calling the three results globally
“high-resolution,” “bounded,” and “early” closure recreates a default hierarchy
and conflicts with scene-local interpretation. Retain only their trusted board
facts globally. Each scene must author its own conduct mapping, and any local
burst may occur outside Exploded.

### RM-05 — Exact resolver signature in a narrative ledger

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** The conceptual signature
`resolve(scene_id, frozen_board_terminal_fact)` prescribes too little context
for saved state and frozen variation while still looking like an implementation
interface. Replace it with a behavioral boundary. Exact function names,
parameters, data shapes, and storage belong to the later technical design.

### RM-06 — Keeping the internal name `affection`

**Classification: Valid trade-off.** The name can mislead future maintainers,
but the owner explicitly chose to avoid rename-only churn. Retain it as an
implementation token, keep “relational momentum” as the canonical design term,
and permit a rename when a substantive state refactor already touches the same
surface.

### RM-07 — Explicit progression windows and conditional non-regression

**Classification: Contract misread.** The existence of sparse, code-owned
windows is confirmed, but their locations and predicates are expressly open
until the plot earns them. Non-regression is conditional on the ordered-state
model surviving. The eventual manual should nevertheless require every window
to correspond to a legible dramatic threshold rather than an arbitrary date.

### RM-08 — Progression timing versus presented consequences

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** Current wording can evaluate
progression before a materially relevant refusal, disclosure, or knowledge
change has actually become canonical. Clarify that every progression input is
fixed and committed before evaluation. A merely presentational atom cannot be
an input; if a variation changes an input, it is a distinct consequence-plan
variant resolved before evaluation.

### RM-09 — Two-to-four family budget

**Classification: Valid trade-off.** A soft target can become a subconscious
quota, but the owner approved the budget and the text already says to collapse
false distinctions. Keep it as a provisional complexity alarm, with permission
for one or more than four only when a later ablation demonstrates that each
reachable continuation is necessary and materially distinct.

### RM-10 — Attention, chance, and parallel-world disclaimers

**Classification: Noise as a design objection; possible editorial compression
later.** The owner explicitly asked for physical-world claim discipline. These
passages prevent a fictional metaphor from being mistaken for a scientific
claim and prevent Observer Pressure from counterfeit input. Repetition may be
compressed in the final manual, but the boundary is load-bearing.

## 6. Style and scene grammar

### ST-01 — Every ending names a later carrier

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** An ending has no later in-run surface.
Require messages and dating scenes to identify any later carrier their durable
consequence needs. Require an ending instead to identify which accumulated
facts it resolves, transforms, or deliberately leaves open.

### ST-02 — Definitive labels and direct declarations

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** A categorical ban can create the coyness
the project rejects. A character may use a relationship label or say “I love
you” when doing so is character-consistent and consequential. The statement is
a character action and evidence, not an author-certified decoding key for every
motive or the relationship's permanent future.

### ST-03 — “Trustworthy signal” and “dangerous counterfeit” table

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** The labels risk becoming a fixed symbol
dictionary. Recast the table as character-specific **legible actions** and
**dangerous alternate uses**. No listed act proves love, and the same action may
carry care, strategy, habit, control, or several at once.

### ST-04 — Irreversible change in every scene

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** “Irreversibly” forces minor messages
and quiet hesitation to overperform. Each included scene should materially
change the immediate relational situation, evidence, opportunity, pressure, or
expectation—or make a costly failure to change newly legible. Only a consequence
that later material consumes needs irreversible carryover.

### ST-05 — First deviation from established behavior

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** Early scenes may still be establishing
a baseline. Ask for the relevant established behavior **or the baseline this
scene establishes**, plus any meaningful deviation that the audience can
actually recognize.

### ST-06 — Mixed physical-world status taxonomy

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** The five current statuses mix source
ontology with confidence. The final manual should record two axes: claim basis
(external-world proposition or fictional stipulation) and epistemic status
(established, supported, open, or contradicted under stated constraints). A
fictional stipulation can then be certain in canon without masquerading as
physics.

### ST-07 — Cost, respect, and repair as love evidence

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** These are possible modes of conduct,
not rungs that authenticate love. Respect for consent is a baseline constraint;
cost may be manipulation; repair may be strategic. Pattern, context, deviation,
and recipient freedom make an act legible without proving an interior state.

### ST-08 — Private action as more trustworthy

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** Conduct continuing without an intended
witness rules out some performative explanations, but privacy does not prove
sincerity. Replace the hierarchy with that narrower inference.

### ST-09 — Rhythm, resistance, misunderstanding, and changed conduct

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** Requiring every exchange to establish
all four produces a repeated rhythm. A scene should be long enough for the
specific pressure and change it carries; these are available diagnostics, not
mandatory beats in every conversation.

### ST-10 — Naming one-use people

**Classification: Valid trade-off.** Excessive anonymity could depopulate the
university, but the owner prefers roles when a one-use name would create false
attention. The existing safeguard is adequate: name anyone whose identity,
agency, relationship, or accountability must be remembered, and let unnamed
people retain local will.

### ST-11 — La Jalousie described as “repetitive and wrong”

**Classification: Valid + actionable in part.** Interpretation may be wrong; it
must not be wrong by authorial decree. Repetition remains useful when it means
the recurrence of exact objects, facts, or altered angles, not duplicated prose
or ornamental cataloguing.

### ST-12 — Frozen Impulse Atom lacks criteria

**Classification: Contract misread.** Section 7.7.4 supplies the criteria,
limits, persistence law, and distinction between cosmetic atoms and material
variants. The scene checklist should cross-reference that section so an
isolated excerpt remains intelligible.

### ST-13 — Named theorists and evidence axioms are redundant

**Classification: Valid trade-off.** The owner requested these lenses and asked
for correction of unsupported physical-world claims. Keep the theorists private
and diagnostic, never imitative or visible in prose. Compress repeated axioms
only after the manual has one authoritative evidence section.

### ST-14 — Vague diagnostic adjectives

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** Terms such as “earned,” “concise at the
level of effect,” “decorative mystery,” and “socially exact” need operational
questions and short positive/negative examples in the finished manual. They
remain identity language, not yet executable scene tests.

## 7. Story foundations

### SF-01 — Over-specified private airport chronology

**Classification: Valid trade-off.** A production-side causal truth prevents
possession, timing, and knowledge contradictions, and the owner approved the
Day 2 airport core. Hidden detail still creates continuity debt. During the
final plot pass, retain only details that constrain a later fact; demote unused
transport, customs, platform, and timing detail to optional staging.

### SF-02 — Guaranteed umbrella facet on every relevant playthrough

**Classification: Valid trade-off.** This discoverability covenant can force a
prop into scenes. The existing narrow eligibility rule mitigates the risk. Test
the guarantee against the actual scene allocation: if any carrier exists only
to satisfy coverage, redesign the carrier or relax the guarantee.

### SF-03 — At most one umbrella facet per carrier

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** A hard numerical ceiling is another
quota. Recast it as a default anti-recap rule: one bounded facet is normally
enough, while two naturally inseparable present facts may coexist if neither
reconstructs the pickup.

### SF-04 — Preferred three-function hint pattern

**Classification: Noise.** The text already calls the pattern a ceiling rather
than a requirement. It is a diagnostic example, not a mandated three-beat
sequence.

### SF-05 — Future cutaway threshold

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** No cutaway belongs to the current game,
and the umbrella design explicitly depends on withholding one. Move the general
future-cutaway advice out of the final Narrative Constitution into a nonbinding
future-ideas or production appendix.

### SF-06 — Hospital presentation surface is unresolved

**Classification: Contract misread.** The ledger already marks this question
open and confines the answer to message, dating scene, or ending. It becomes a
defect only if the plot is approved without allocating the Hospital consequence
to one or more of those existing classes.

### SF-07 — “Knowingly” crossing hidden Hospital limits

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** Hidden variables do not support a claim
of knowingly crossing both limits unless play supplies legible warnings about
the joint risk. Use “audience-caused” as the baseline. Moral culpability or
deliberate neglect must be earned by the evidence available before the final
operation, not inferred from omniscient documentation.

### SF-08 — “Non-compensatory consequence” is ambiguous

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** Define it mechanically: Hospitalization
does not award relational momentum, valid consent, romance, or an automatic
offsetting reward merely because it grants access to Sylvia. It need not assert
one author-certified moral interpretation of the audience.

### SF-09 — Fixed private motives in the umbrella core

**Classification: Valid trade-off.** Coherent character causality benefits from
writer-side motive commitments even when the audience cannot prove them. Keep
Lavinia's wish for presence and Priscilla's care/control mixture private and
fallibly expressed; no carrier or narrator may certify either as the only full
explanation of the women.

### SF-10 — “Retain useful foundations” as a canon loophole

**Classification: Valid + actionable.** “Useful” alone cannot authorize an old
claim. Require every inherited premise used by the new plot to be traceable to
a source and explicitly marked retained, reopened, superseded, or newly
confirmed.

### SF-11 — Totally Dark limited to a named pattern list

**Classification: Valid trade-off.** Concrete patterns keep Dark from becoming
a synonym for intensity or aesthetic danger. Treat the list as non-exhaustive,
but require any addition to identify equally concrete, knowingly reinforced
conduct and its agency or consent consequence.

### SF-12 — Website and reconciliation workflow inside the ledger

**Classification: Contract misread for the working ledger.** This artifact is a
decision ledger whose purpose includes provenance and later reconciliation, so
the material belongs here now. When the final Narrative Constitution and Scene
Manual are compiled, move production workflow and website debt to their own
records rather than presenting them as story law.

## 8. Load-bearing rules that survived removal pressure

The first pass found no reason to remove these foundations:

- exactly three current narrative surface classes;
- separation of audience knowledge, Angela knowledge, and private author truth;
- nondiegetic boards with truthful, frozen input and replay behavior;
- one run-frozen, unseen-first Priscilla–Lavinia state/tone combination that
  pair-board play cannot mutate;
- scene-local authored consequences rather than globally moralized outcomes;
- no mechanic may manufacture another character's feeling, desire, consent, or
  private motive;
- Hospitalization requires both the pressure and health boundaries;
- the umbrella event exists offscreen and is inferred through bounded facets;
- progression and ending evaluation remain code-owned and presentation does not
  calculate eligibility; and
- physical possibility, empirical support, and fictional stipulation remain
  distinct.

## 9. Resolved owner distinction

The phrase “there is no way to change Priscilla and Lavinia's relationship” now
means that each new playthrough freezes one of four categorical forms:
Ambiguous + Sweet, Ambiguous + Dark, Love + Sweet, or Love + Dark. Pair-board
results, Angela, and other in-run operations cannot change those axes. The pair's
own decisions may still change trust, distance, damage, repair, knowledge,
obligation, and future conduct without converting the frozen category.

The profile excludes an actually witnessed combination from later new-run draws
until all four have first been witnessed. A hidden draw does not count. After
the one-time discovery cycle is complete, later new playthroughs draw freely
from all four combinations; the exclusion cycle does not restart.

## 10. Application record

The 2026-09-03 reconciliation applied the actionable repairs to the working
ledger: pair-selector vocabulary, scene-local board meaning, consequence/evaluator
timing, removal of a premature resolver signature, love-signal non-certainty,
direct declarations, flexible scene tests, two-axis evidence classification,
Hospital warning knowledge, umbrella-facet flexibility, selective-reset
traceability, and final-manual separation of future-cutaway advice.

The accepted trade-offs remain documented rather than silently erased. The
four-state pair law was added to both the narrative ledger and future runtime
behavior record. The owner subsequently settled its post-discovery policy as a
free draw from all four combinations. This concludes ablation cycle 1; the
revised pair cluster can receive one fresh-context check with no repeat-policy
question outstanding.
