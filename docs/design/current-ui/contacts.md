## 2. Product character, scope, and non-goals

Contacts is an ordinary university correspondence register whose causal
behavior is exact while its records may feel quietly impossible. It presents
correspondence, order, time, direction, and absence as evidence. It never says
what that evidence means, names an anomaly as an anomaly, or manufactures a
fictional malfunction. Audience interpretation remains outside the interface.

The application must always feel competent and trustworthy:

- Focus, open, read, witness, accept, reply, expire, save, and load are distinct
  operations with exact boundaries.
- An accepted command is never corrupted to create horror or uncertainty.
- Absence appears as absence, not as a warning, deletion scar, placeholder, or
  explanatory clue.
- Contacts replies and invitation operations are application operations, not
  selectable spoken-scene narrative choices.
- Minesweeper results remain the game's sole choice-like narrative agency.

Contacts has no arbitrary composer, free typing, Send field, attachment,
reaction, voice message, block function, typing indicator, online or last-seen
status, social feed, relationship meter, route preview, or hidden-stat display.
It contains no horror-red alert, glitch wipe, fake crash, decorative error code,
modern messenger bubble cloud, generic Dating inbox, or supernatural-terminal
label. Final story prose, relationship outcomes, invitation calendars,
Hospital, endings, Gallery, Observer, and audio remain externally owned.

Its styling also forbids terminal-log sterility; ornamental calligraphy,
talismans, seals, or generic East-Asian mysticism; flicker, scanlines, RGB
split, fake compression, layout jitter, missing glyphs, corrupted timestamps,
and changing hitboxes. Roughness must read as an old university OS rendered
through ink and photocopy, never as broken software or cheap manufactured
hardware.

## 3. Domain vocabulary and ownership

The following terms are exact:

- **Delivered entry:** a canonical message, follow-up, invitation, or linked
  entry currently available to one contact.
- **Focused row:** the row holding keyboard, gamepad, or assistive Focus. Focus
  is presentation only.
- **Open thread:** the single friend conversation projected in the right pane.
- **Bulk-read boundary:** one accepted friend activation that marks every entry
  admitted through an exact delivery sequence as read.
- **Reply boundary:** one accepted authored operational response beneath its
  source entry.
- **Presentation watermark:** the highest delivered sequence admitted by a
  successful bulk-read command. It is never merely friend plus day.
- **Witnessed presentation:** a separate receipt that proves an entry was
  actually presented visually or through accepted assistive traversal.
- **Unanswered ordinary entry:** an ordinary message whose A/B/C response has
  not committed, regardless of whether it has been opened.
- **Contacts history:** the visible chronological correspondence in one thread;
  it is not global narrative History, profile visited-line state, a transaction
  receipt, or Observer evidence.
- **View cache:** same-day, noncanonical selected-thread, scroll, and Focus
  presentation state.

The Contacts domain owns entries, canonical sequence, unread/read state,
acceptance, replies, closure, private tombstones, immutable semantic IDs, and
idempotent receipts. Contacts UI owns only their projection, selection,
scrolling, Focus, and transient notification content/lifetime behavior.
Shared input semantics make pointer click, short touch tap, keyboard Accept,
gamepad Accept, and assistive activation the same command. Hover is inspection
only; Back uses the shared cancel/back semantic route.

Here `Accept` is keyboard Enter/Space through the shared UI map, controller
south/A-equivalent, touch tap, pointer primary click, or assistive activation.
Focus order remains semantically identical across keyboard, controller, and
assistive traversal. Contacts binds no gameplay-only board action directly.

Sylvia. Rows never sort by activity. Their only public facts are the literal
localized first name and binary Unread when present. Identity slits are
redundant styling of the same public name, not a third fact.

Incoming slips are Worn Cream on Worn Cream, established by left alignment,

It receives no identity colour, slit, portrait, group badge, reply arrow, or

Entries sort only by stable monotonic canonical sequence, never localized text,
sender, timestamp, dictionary order, node order, device time, or clock bleed.
The accepted daily stack fixes that sequence as previous-day causal follow-up,
then today's fixed ordinary message when present, then newly unlocked
invitation. This order is canonical input, not something the UI derives.

After bulk read, the transcript aligns the oldest newly read entry with a small
margin. A bilateral contextual-dark rule labelled with the localized equivalent
of `NEW` appears immediately before it. `NEW` is a temporary nonfocusable
session boundary with exact semantic role `session_new_boundary`, not Unread,
Information, a count, or narrative evidence. It
participates in transcript reading order, creates no witnessed receipt, is
never saved, and disappears under the accepted leave/reopen/cache law.

Top/bottom continuation uses a quiet contextual-dark cut-notch only while
content is genuinely offscreen. Notches have no nodes and convey only overflow.
There is no arrow, fade, percentage, pulse, or decorative animation. The
transcript owns one scroll region and exposes ordinary scroll position and
commands; wheel, trackpad, touch drag, keyboard, and gamepad scrolling never
alter read state.

When nothing is newly read, reopen priority is: valid cache and meaningful
Focus; otherwise earliest unresolved inline action; otherwise latest entry.
Immediately after a bulk read, Focus stays on the transcript/oldest-new anchor,
never on response A or an invitation operation.
Tab/Right enters the earliest unresolved operational response. Back/Left
returns to the selected row. Focus never wraps into another friend implicitly.

## 7. Focus, activation, bulk reading, and witnessing

Focus, Hover, assistive inspection, scrolling, locale or text-size change,
window refocus, and visual selection styling never open a thread or read an
entry. Only explicit activation does.

Activating a friend atomically:

1. snapshots the exact highest delivered sequence admitted for that friend;
2. opens the thread;
3. marks every entry through that sequence read;
4. commits each entry-specific consequence of reading; and
5. projects the transcript at the oldest newly read entry.

The command identity contains friend plus admitted delivery boundary. A
friend/day-only ID is forbidden because a later same-day arrival must remain
openable through a new idempotent command.

A solo invitation below the fold is accepted causally at bulk read even while
still unwitnessed. Read and witnessed presentation are separate. Witness occurs
exactly once when an entry's leading content enters an accepted laid-out
viewport stream with its complete text ordinarily reachable, or when semantic
assistive traversal/TTS explicitly presents it. No dwell time is required.
Bulk read, node creation, or offscreen layout alone never witnesses it.

Witness receipts satisfy only explicitly registered witnessed-line, echo, or
presentation-atom contracts. Generic Contacts inspection never infers Observer
evidence, acceptance, reply, relationship state, or an outcome.

An entry delivered to an already-open thread remains held and unread. The row
shows binary Unread, while the new slip, actions, sender/time/text nodes, layout
allocation, TTS, live announcement, accessibility node, and witness receipt are
absent. Only row reactivation, that entry's valid toast `Go`, or hiding and
reopening Contacts through the launcher creates a new open boundary. Scrolling,
Focus, resize, language, or refocus cannot reveal it.

Reactivation admits the new delivery boundary, clears only the admitted Unread
fact, publishes the entry at the oldest-new anchor, and moves Focus under the
transcript law. A launcher reopen of a cached thread performs this only for that
friend; entries for the other two friends remain unread.

## 8. Ordinary responses and invitations

Exactly six ordinary messages are replyable under the external calendar.
Opening one never chooses an answer. An unresolved source owns exactly three
authored operational response stamps directly beneath it. There is no composer
or fake editable field. Each stamp remains at least 48×48 logical pixels and
wraps within the transcript.

Each stamp uses Worn Cream, contextual-dark text, and a stamped boundary. Its
authored response text is the complete action label; it receives no extra
category badge.

Response stamps are app operations, not moral cards, route selectors, result
grades, Minesweeper outcomes, or spoken-scene choices. They use paper Focus,
Hover, and Press rather than Selected. Inspection reveals no stat effect,
preferred answer, danger, tone grade, consequence, or future echo.

Activating A, B, or C atomically records stable reply and witnessed-line IDs,
removes all three controls with zero geometry/nodes, inserts Angela's authored
outgoing slip, inserts the scripted incoming response, creates one accepted
echo obligation, and resolves Focus to the committed response or transcript
anchor. Saved text is bounded, plain, escaped, and nonexecutable. The command is
stat-neutral and idempotent.

There is no retained chosen-answer colour, `Sending…`, `Sent`, Busy,
Success, check, toast, celebration, or delayed promise. New correspondence is
the complete result. A real failure leaves the trustworthy old transcript and
controls intact.

Opening a thread containing a solo invitation is acceptance and Schedule
addability. It projects the scripted outgoing acceptance as ordinary
correspondence and adds no Accept/Decline UI, confirmation, badge, special
colour, or acceptance toast. Acceptance makes the date addable; it does not
schedule the date.

Priscilla-Lavinia is the only linked invitation exception with response
controls. It creates no fourth contact or group toast. Both rows receive
ordinary Unread and are read independently. The first open participant becomes
the inviter; one shared action accepts exactly once. A later response in the
other participant's projection may add only registered authored variation and
cannot accept or schedule twice. Pair autonomy, counted-window law, and expiry
branches remain externally owned.

The first committed linked response updates both thread projections. Merely
opening the second participant creates no reply, judgment variation,
acceptance, or Schedule mutation; that participant's response remains
uncommitted until activated. The fixed inviter, presentation order, and shared
receipts survive save/load, reopen, replay, rapid activation, and concurrent
attempts without racing into different identities.

## 9. Notifications and Schedule routing

The closed toast eligibility table is:

| Delivery | Toast |
|---|---|
| one of six ordinary messages | yes |
| a Days-1–6 solo invitation, or an eligible Day-7 solo invitation, after its no-faint publication boundary | yes |
| next-day causal follow-up or institutional notice first delivered in safe desktop state | yes |
| scripted response in an open exchange | no |
| Angela outgoing or scripted acceptance slip | no |
| Priscilla-Lavinia activation/update | no |
| Day-2 pre-echo pair | no |
| Hospital, ending, History-only, echo-only, technical, or recovery record | no |

Safe desktop state begins only after delivery and condition/no-faint evaluation
commit, the desktop owns input, and no Hospital, ending, technical recovery, or
other blocking route is pending. Earlier eligible delivery queues without
changing canonical sequence.

An atomic eligible batch publishes in ascending canonical order. Each newer
toast replaces the visible prior toast; all underlying Unread remains. Only the
final visible toast announces politely. Toast content is exactly `New message`,
friend first name, `Go`, and quiet `X`: no preview, time, type, invitation hint,
count, portrait, sound requirement, or route explanation.

Its polite live announcement contains only `New message` and the friend's first
name; it does not announce `Go`, `X`, or hidden delivery facts.

The rendered toast is the shell-owned controlled-dark Information plate. It is
outside Contacts component/state ownership and receives no friend slit or
colour, Unread morphology, timer bar, or response action.

The toast binds exact run, logical day, friend, entry ID, and delivery sequence.
Before `Go`, the coordinator revalidates existence and Unread. Load, New Run,
day transition, expiry, supersession, or another read closes stale binding
quietly without navigation or mutation.

Lifetime is twenty seconds of eligible monotonic foreground UI time. It pauses
while the game lacks Focus, a trusted modal blocks, or `Go`/`X` holds Focus. A
replacement starts a full interval. Arrival never steals Focus. Deliberate
Tab/Shift+Tab or controller shoulders enter its two-control group; Back performs
`X`. `X`/timeout dismiss presentation only. `Go` invokes the ordinary open
boundary. Failed navigation reads nothing.

`X` returns Focus to the previously meaningful control. Successful `Go`
transfers Focus to the target transcript under the ordinary oldest-new law.

Replacement preserves the same semantic control Focus where possible and
announces the new friend once; otherwise it restores the saved meaningful
control. Toasts queue until a safe nonoverlapping shell region exists and never
cover an active modal, Focused control, transcript, caption stack, or essential
status. They never contribute to witnessed receipts.

The shell owns plate placement, overlay priority, safe regions, and host Focus
restoration. Contacts owns eligibility, binding, copy, lifetime, and `Go`/`X`
meaning. Toast presentation is transient and never restored from save.
Save, Load, and launcher reopen reconstruct canonical Unread facts but never
resurrect an expired, dismissed, suppressed, or replaced toast.

Schedule warning deliberately opens the bare list, Focuses `Contacts list`,
reads/accepts/restores nothing, and overwrites only the same-day view cache. Its
warning eligibility/order and causal routing remain Schedule-owned.
The successful route consumes only Schedule's own warning receipt and changes
no correspondence fact.

## 10. Time, anomaly, expiry, and absolute erasure

Routine time uses compact tabular 24-hour `HH:MM`. Authored time and canonical
order never depend on localized text or the audience clock. One routine slip
owns one marker beneath it; only a registered contiguous minute group replaces
duplicates with one shared marker. That grouping is identified by stable
`minute_group_id` and never changes semantic order or creates causality.

Routine timestamps stay beneath their owning slips on Worn Cream; none moves
inside a Bruised Plum outgoing slip. Localized story-day separators and minute
groups use the same contextual-dark paper grammar. Save/load, timezone or DST
change, manual clock edits, sleep/resume, locale change, and low frame rate
cannot rewrite an authored timestamp. Assistive traversal announces a shared
minute once at its group boundary.

The Day-2 pair uses one routine shared marker immediately before its first slip.
A rare Contacts clock bleed uses the exact same component; only frozen displayed
digit glyphs differ. It has no status, warning, icon, tooltip, glitch, sound,
identity change, provenance, or mechanic. It cannot affect sorting, grouping,
read state, deadlines, actions, routes, relationships, echoes, or endings.
